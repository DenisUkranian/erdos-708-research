# Arbitrary prices on subpower primes and a necessary condition at polynomial scales

Let J=[1,M] and let I be any interval of M consecutive positive integers. Write E_K for the uniform mean on K. Prices are in [0,1], with X(t)=sum_{p<=M} w_p v_p(t).

**Theorem.** Suppose that along a sequence M tending to infinity, for every fixed eta>0 and 0<delta<1/2,

    sum_{M^eta < p <= M, delta <= w_p <= 1-delta} 1/p --> 0.

Then, uniformly over shifts,

    E_I min(X,1) <= E_J min(X,2) + o(1).

In particular there is no price restriction at any subpower scale. For an arbitrary-shift, positive-density sequence violating the asymptotic by a fixed factor, the covering dual must have, on a subsequence, fixed eta, delta, c>0 for which the sum above exceeds c. This is only a necessary condition. The theorem does not control profiles carrying such mass.

## Uniform geometric model for the small-prime part

Let y tend to infinity with log y = o(log M). For every fixed a>0, uniformly on positive intervals of length Z >= M^a, the full vector of exponents at primes p<=y is within o(1) in total variation of independent geometric exponents G_p with Pr(G_p>=j)=p^(-j). Exponents of targets can be arbitrarily large.

Indeed, write D=product p^{G_p} and V=product_{p<=y}(1-1/p). For y-smooth d, Pr(D=d)=V/d. The mean of log D is sum log p/(p-1) = O(log y), so Pr(D>sqrt Z)=o(1) uniformly. For y-smooth d<=sqrt Z, the count of integers in the interval with full smooth part exactly d is the sifted count of integers coprime to the primes <=y in a quotient interval of length T=Z/d up to endpoint rounding. The dimension-one fundamental lemma, with level sqrt(T), gives this count as TV(1+o(1)) uniformly. More explicitly the error is o(TV)+O(sqrt(T)), since each squarefree divisibility count differs from T divided by its modulus by at most one, the sieve weights are bounded by one, and log(sqrt(T))/log y tends to infinity uniformly. Mertens' estimate gives sqrt(T)=o(TV). Summing the common lower bound (1-o(1))V/d over d<=sqrt Z shows that the two probability laws have overlap 1-o(1). See [Koukoulopoulos, The Distribution of Prime Numbers, Theorem 19.1](https://dms.umontreal.ca/~koukoulo/documents/publications/primes.pdf).

## Binary prices above y

First assume all prices above y are zero or one. The [mixed binary proof](../cofactor_flow_2026_11_26/MIXED_BINARY_PRICES.md) gives the claimed comparison once its core moment estimates are verified. We give the replacements explicitly. The large-reciprocal-mass proof is unchanged and does not require any core. Otherwise the sum of reciprocals over active primes above y is bounded by 3-sqrt(3). Let s be the small-prime load and h=(1-s)_+. The means of min(s,1) on I and J differ by o(1) by the total variation estimate. Let m be the geometric model mean of h. For a squarefree product d of up to three active primes and d<=M^(1-b), with b>0 fixed, the multiples of d in either interval parametrize a quotient interval of length at least M^b-1. The smooth prime exponents in each multiple are those of its quotient since d is coprime to the core. Hence, on each interval, the normalized h-weighted divisibility count is m/d+o(1)/d+O(1/M), uniformly in d. The sum of reciprocals of the allowed products is bounded in terms of the total reciprocal mass and their bounded multiplicities. Their count below M^(1-b) is O(M^(1-b)). Thus the errors sum to o(1). For d>M^(1-b) the boundary estimate of the mixed binary proof uses only 0<=h<=1, bounded reciprocal mass, and a prime factor >M^(1/4) for b<1/4. Its total error is O(b)+o(1). Sending b to zero proves the comparison by the same ordered moment inequalities. Their mesh condition is automatic since 1/y tends to zero.

## Reduction of the general price condition

Choose integers j=j(M) increasing sufficiently slowly that j<=sqrt(log M) and the reciprocal sum in the hypothesis at eta=delta=1/j is o(1). Set y=M^(1/j). Beyond y, primes with 1/j<=w_p<=1-1/j meet only o(M) positions in either interval, by the union bound sum(1/p+1/M) and pi(M)=o(M). Removing them changes either capped mean by o(1).

The set of positions divisible by p^2 for any remaining p>y likewise has proportion at most sum_{p>y}1/p^2+pi(M)/M=o(1), uniformly on both intervals. Outside these exceptions, first powers suffice. The expected load of all primes above y priced below 1/j is at most j^(-1) sum_{y<p<=M}(1/p+1/M)=O((1+log j)/j)=o(1) by Mertens. Finally rounding each remaining price above 1-1/j to one changes either cap by at most 2/j once j>=3: three occurrences already saturate both caps, and up to two lose at most 2/j in total. The resulting profile is binary beyond the subpower core, where the preceding result applies.

## Connection to covering

For completeness, when |A|=n is bounded below by a fixed multiple of M, LP rounding costs at most pi(M)=o(n) positions. At a capped prime-price vector the covering-dual objective is bounded by 2n plus the excess of the source hinge above 2 over the target hinge above 1. The entire target interval supplies at least the canonical interval's total valuation of each prime since its count of each prime-power multiple is at least floor(M/p^j). Hence this hinge excess is at most M times the difference of the two capped means in the theorem. The ambient project supplies the price truncation and boxed-dual reductions. No relative o(n) claim follows in the sparse regime.

## Unrestricted fractional prices at the top scales

There is a second sufficient condition, independent of the first. Allow arbitrary prices on the subpower core p<=y and on a set Q of primes p>M^a for fixed a>0, with zero prices elsewhere. Suppose on all but o(M) canonical rows the sum of prices of the occurring Q-primes after one largest price is deleted is at most one. Then the same cap comparison holds. In particular it holds with completely unrestricted prices on all p>M^(1/3) together with an arbitrary subpower core. One may also use any integer r>=2, threshold p>M^(1/(r+1)), and prices at most 1/(r-1). A different sufficient bound on Q is w_p<=log p/log(M/p) for p<M, interpreting this as no restriction for p=M. Indeed delete a largest prime P; the prices of the remaining primes add to at most log(M/P)/log(M/P)=1 when P<M. Deleting a largest price only improves this bound.

To prove the claim set s to the core load and h=(1-s)_+. Except on a proportion at most sum_{p>M^a}1/p^2+pi(M)/M=o(1), a Q-prime occurs only to the first power in a row, on either interval. Pointwise the target cap is bounded by min(s,1) plus the sum of min(w_p,h) over the distinct Q-primes present, up to a capped error at exceptional rows. On a good canonical row the latter sum is at most 1+h, by bounding its largest-price term by h, and is at most the macroscopic load. Therefore the canonical cap at 2 dominates that expression when s<=1. For s>=1 the claimed domination follows from h=0. Every canonical row carries at most 1/a macroscopic occurrences, so the exceptional canonical rows cost o(1) in the comparison.

The two means of min(s,1) agree up to o(1). For each p<=M^(1-b) in Q the bounded smooth-profile function min(w_p,h) on the p-multiples has, by the geometric model above, the same mean on both quotient intervals to within o(1). The sum of reciprocals over Q is bounded because a>0 is fixed. The count error O(1/M) per prime sums to o(1); for p>M^(1-b) the total contribution is O(b)+o(1) by Mertens and pi(M)=o(M). Send b to zero. For the cutoff M^(1/3), three prime factors beyond the cutoff cannot occur in a canonical row. At the cutoff M^(1/(r+1)) there are at most r factors, giving the stated price bound.

## A prefix comparison with one spare prime

The following result allows a wider band of completely unrestricted prices. Keep the arbitrary subpower core. Let all positive prices outside the core be on a set Q contained in (M^a,M], where a>0 is fixed, and assume

    sum_{p in Q, p<=sqrt(M)} 1/p <= 1+o(1).

Then the cap comparison holds for every such price vector and every shift. In particular Q can contain all primes p>M^(1/(2e)), since the reciprocal sum up to sqrt(M) is log(e)+o(1). The claim has no bound on the prices within Q other than 0<=w_p<=1. Only first occurrences of primes in Q need to be used in the proof: the normalized capped error of discarding powers is o(1) on both intervals by the square-divisibility bound above.

Let s be the core load. Rows with s>=1 contribute exactly one on the target and at least one on the source, and their frequencies agree to o(1). For s<1 put h=1-s. Subtract the equivalent common core contributions s on these rows. For each of the two remaining caps express min(W,c)=W-(W-c)_+, where W is the Q-load of the row, taking c=h on I and c=1+h on J. The means of the linear parts restricted to s<1 agree up to o(1), by the single-prime geometric-profile comparison (sum 1/p over Q is bounded). It suffices to bound the source hinge increments from above by the target increments. Process primes in increasing order. The hinge increment at q equals the indicator of q dividing the row times the integral, over 0<=z<=w_q, of the indicator that the load of the earlier primes exceeds c-z (ignoring a single endpoint of integration). For fixed s,z put u=h-z. When u<0 the target indicator is one whenever q divides the row; the geometric comparison handles this part of the integral with only q. It remains to compare the source event 'earlier load >1+u' to the target event 'earlier load >u' for u>=0.

For each counted canonical row containing q take the shortest prefix B of its present earlier primes, in increasing order, with total price greater than u. This prefix exists, and omits at least one more present prime p0, because removing the largest earlier prime loses at most one. With r=max B and d=q times the product of B, we have r<p0<q and dp0<=M. For a fixed possible B, write L_B for the Q-primes r<p<q with dp<=M. The source indicator assigned to B is bounded by the sum over p in L_B of the indicators of dp dividing the row.

On the target require d dividing the row and absence of each prime in E_B={p in Q:p<r and p not in B}. When the price sum of B without r is <=u and the price sum of B exceeds u, this event describes B as the unique shortest present prefix crossing u. Such target events are disjoint over B for fixed q,s,z. Retain only B with nonempty L_B. Any p in E_B satisfies dp<=M, since p<r and L_B has a larger element. The Bonferroni lower bound for the target event is the indicator for d minus the sum of the indicators for dp with p in E_B.

In the ideal independent core comparison, the source upper bound for B is (sum over L_B of 1/p)/d, and the target lower bound is (1-sum over E_B of 1/p)/d. Both receive the same nonnegative factor, namely the geometric average and z-integral of the core and prefix eligibility indicators. Every p in these two disjoint sets is <q and satisfies pq<=M, so p<sqrt(M). The reciprocal-mass assumption gives the desired order up to o(1)/d.

For precision in applying the comparisons, every d or dp in this argument is <=M and involves at most floor(1/a) distinct macroscopic primes. A given product occurs a bounded number of times (its prime subset B and its distinguished primes q,p have boundedly many choices). Each coefficient obtained after z-integration is a bounded function of the core profile. Their reciprocal sum is bounded by the exponential of the total Q reciprocal mass, times a constant depending on a. For moduli <=M^(1-b) the geometric core comparison therefore incurs total error o(1) for fixed b>0. Moduli above M^(1-b) carry total reciprocal mass O_a(b)+o(1): fix all prime factors but one; the remaining prime is >M^a and lies between M^(1-b) divided by the fixed product and M divided by it. Mertens gives reciprocal mass O_a(b)+o(1) uniformly there, and the fixed products have bounded reciprocal sum. The number of all these moduli is O_a(M/log M) by summing the prime-counting upper bound pi(M/e) over such fixed products e (requiring the remaining prime >M^a). Each count divided by M is at most 1/d+1/M. Taking b to zero completes the proof including all boundary costs.
