# Disjoint bins from the ascending prime prefix

Let A be a set of n distinct integers ≥2 with maximum M. An atom of a source a is one of its full prime powers p^{v_p(a)}. Delete its numerically largest atom q and put d=a/q. All prime divisors of d are ≤q≤u=M/d. Assign each nonunit cofactor d to a pool P containing every prime factor of d. The deleted atom may use any prime. Permit arbitrarily many pools, with the requirement that different pools are disjoint as sets of primes. The same pool may serve many rows.

For each pool separately and a positive target t, multiply its P-prime factors in increasing order, with repetitions, stopping before the first multiplication giving a product greater than M. Use 1 for the empty prefix. Call the resulting product its bin. Each target t=dv for which v has no P-prime divisor ≤u belongs to bin d. Indeed all P-primes coming from v are larger than every prime of d, and even the first such prime would push the prefix product above M. The quotients v range over the integers in an interval of real length u. Different d in the same pool give disjoint bins. At most Q(u) source rows have the same d, where Q counts prime powers through u, since a=dq. In particular Q(u)=π(u)+O(√u log(2u)).

**Bin criterion.** Suppose that for some fixed U≥2, for every pool and each real u≥U, every positive-integer quotient interval cut out by a half-open real interval of length u starting at a nonnegative real number contains at least Q(u)+1 integers free of that pool's prime factors ≤u. Then for all sufficiently large M, uniformly in the shift, n and source density, H≤2n−1. It suffices to check the actual capacities of the occupied bins against their own row counts plus one.

Every occupied u tends uniformly to infinity as M tends to infinity. Indeed u<U implies every full prime-power atom of a is at most U, so d divides lcm(1,...,ceil U) and u≥M/lcm(1,...,ceil U). This excludes u<U for large enough M. Anchor one arbitrary source at an actual multiple in the interval, remove that source and reserve its chosen position. Within each pool, match all remaining nonunit cofactors injectively, avoiding the reserved position; the plus-one slack pays for it. Unit cofactors need no representative. Selections from different pools may overlap. For each given prime, all credited cofactor valuations occur within just one pool, where the selected representatives are distinct. Thus the union meets all prime-power level counts except for at most the missing atoms, at most one per unanchored source. For each prime, repair its nested power levels by selecting positions of greatest remaining valuation, stopping when all counts for that prime are met. The whole interval contains at least the required count at every level and the cost is at most one additional position per outstanding atom. The anchored row's levels have a distinct credited position. The final size is ≤1+(n−1)+(n−1)=2n−1.

**Finite reciprocal condition.** Suppose the pools and their number may vary with M, but each pool satisfies Σ_{p∈P}1/p≤1−ε with a common fixed ε>0. The number of permissible quotients is at least u−1−Σ_{p∈P,p≤u}(u/p+1)≥εu−1−π(u), more than Q(u)+1 for all sufficiently large u in terms of ε. Thus the criterion is uniform over these pool families, even when the total reciprocal mass of their union grows. More generally, for a fixed pool with convergent reciprocal sum of any size, remove finitely many small primes first: if P₀ is the initial finite set, with V₀=∏_{P₀}(1−1/p), pre-sieving loses at most O(∏_{P₀}p) from the V₀u main term, uniformly in the interval. Since V₀ is bounded below by a positive pool-dependent constant, choose P₀ so that the remaining reciprocal sum is below V₀/2. Subtracting the remaining multiples leaves ≫_P u. The same holds for finitely many such fixed disjoint pools by taking the maximum of the finitely many thresholds.

## Local pools for conflicting cofactors

There is a more flexible criterion that dispenses with the disjoint support assumption. For each distinct nonunit cofactor d, choose a blocking set P_d of primes as follows. For every distinct larger occupied cofactor e>d with gcd(d,e)>1, require P_d to contain at least one prime dividing e/gcd(e,d). Such a prime exists because e>d, and each such prime is at most the largest prime divisor of e, hence at most M/e≤M/d. The P_d may vary with d and overlap arbitrarily; they need not include the prime factors of d itself.

A target b=dv whose quotient has no P_d-prime ≤M/d cannot be a multiple of any larger conflicting e. In fact if e|b, then e/gcd(e,d)|v. Consequently two representatives chosen by distinct cofactors can coincide only when those cofactors are coprime. Within each d inject the rows as before. This makes all cofactor credits involving a given prime distinct, so the same anchor and repair proof applies. It is sufficient that every local blocking pool satisfy Σ_{p∈P_d}1/p≤1−ε for common fixed ε, or any common quotient-capacity bound from the criterion. To use the residue-class result it suffices that every local pool lie in one of finitely many allowed thin residue-class pools; these larger pools may now overlap. If one has disjoint source pools as above, choose the corresponding whole pool as P_d, since a conflicting e must then belong to the same pool. A large connected cofactor incidence graph can therefore still satisfy the local test.

Sources having at most two distinct prime factors can also be left out of the entire cofactor test. When there is at least one tested row, anchor a tested row and use at most one ordinary repair point per full atom of each omitted source, costing at most two per omitted source. If all sources have at most two atoms, anchor any one and repair the others directly. More generally an omitted set E costs at most the sum of its atom counts; this gives an excess over the two-per-row budget of at most the sum of (ω(a)−2) over E, provided that a tested row remains for the anchor.

Even the primality requirement on the blockers is optional for a finite computation: remove from the d-quotient interval the multiples of e/gcd(e,d) for each such e>d. If the remaining quotient count is at least one more than the count of rows of cofactor d for every occupied d, one obtains the same exact result. Selecting a prime blocker replaces this union of multiples by a tractable superset.

## A growing number of local blockers

Fix 0≤b<1/(1+√e). If each local blocking set satisfies |P_d|=O((M/d)^b), with a common implied constant, then H≤2n−1 uniformly for sufficiently large M. In particular it suffices that each cofactor d share a prime with at most (log M)^b distinct larger cofactors after the rank-one and rank-two source rows have been removed. Select one blocker per such larger cofactor. This criterion does not bound the number of primes in any individual source or the total number of interacting cofactor labels.

Indeed u=M/d satisfies u≫log M uniformly: every atom of a is at most u, so log a≤log lcm(1,...,floor u)=ψ(u)=O(u), while M=ud≤ua. Choose a fixed t with max(b,1/4)<t<1/(1+√e). On any interval of real length u the lower linear sieve through y=u^t counts at least

    [2 log(1/t−1)+o(1)] u/log u

integers without any prime divisor ≤y, uniformly in the shift. Specifically, the interval form of Jurkat–Richert gives u V_all(y)(f(1/t)+o(1)), where V_all(y)~exp(−γ)/log y and f(s)=2 exp(γ) log(s−1)/s for 2<s≤4. The divisor-count remainders are at most one. See [Granville, *Sieving intervals and Siegel zeros*, equation (1) and the differential relations on page 2](https://dms.umontreal.ca/~andrew/PDF/Sieve.Remark.20.09.26.pdf), in particular the unconditional interval estimate immediately before Corollary 1. Real endpoints affect the count by at most a constant. The leading coefficient strictly exceeds one by the choice of t. Removing the multiples of all primes in P_d above y costs at most O(u^b (u/y+1))=o(u/log u), even with shifts chosen adversarially. The survivors exceed Q(u)+1 for all large u, as required.

## A pool of positive prime density

There is an absolute δ₀>0 such that the same exact 2n−1 conclusion holds when each largest-atom cofactor is contained in one of a fixed finite collection of disjoint pools, each a union of reduced residue classes modulo a fixed m of relative prime density 0<δ<δ₀. Finitely many primes may be added while keeping the pools disjoint. A pool may consequently have divergent reciprocal sum. This theorem imposes no limit on any source's number of prime factors or their multiplicities. The threshold for u may depend on the fixed pool. The resulting statement concerns a restricted family of sources.

Here are the analytic details. Write V(z)=∏_{p∈P,p≤z}(1−1/p). Mertens' theorem for fixed arithmetic progressions gives V(z)~c_P(log z)^(-δ); PNT in those progressions gives prime density δ. Use the dimension-one fundamental lemma of sieve theory, with the upper estimate on dimension furnished by all primes. There is an absolute fixed K>2 such that on any interval of real length H, the count after sifting primes in P through H^(1/K) lies between H V(H^(1/K))/2 and 2 H V(H^(1/K)), for all large H, uniformly in the shift and the subset of primes. To see the uniformity, the product condition in the fundamental lemma is bounded by the corresponding full-prime product and has absolute constant. The divisibility remainders are bounded by one for each squarefree modulus. Taking sieve level H^(1/2) makes their total O(H^(1/2+o(1))), negligible relative to H V(H^(1/K))≫H/log H. The fundamental-lemma relative error tends to zero as K increases. See [Kevin Ford, *Sieve Methods Lecture Notes*, fundamental lemma](https://ford126.web.illinois.edu/sieve2023.pdf).

Set z=u^(1/K). After the lower sieve through z, subtract the survivors divisible by each p∈P with z<p≤u. On dividing by such a p the quotient interval has length H=u/p. Since p>z and H^(1/K)≤z, the upper sieve on that interval through H^(1/K) bounds the discarded count. Fix any sufficiently large H₁ depending on the pool and an arbitrarily small η>0. For H≥H₁, Mertens' estimate gives, uniformly,

    V(H^(1/K))/V(z) ≤ (1+η)(log u / log H)^δ.

For H<H₁ use H₁+1 per relevant p, giving O_{H₁}(u/log u)=o(u V(z)) when δ<1. The normalized upper loss for all larger H is at most

    2(1+η) Σ_{z<p≤u/H₁, p∈P} (1/p)(1−log p/log u)^(−δ).

This sum tends to δ times the integral of (1−t)^(−δ)/t from 1/K to 1. On any truncated range through 1−ε this follows from PNT in fixed classes and partial summation. For the omitted range split H between consecutive powers of 2. For H∈[2^j,2^(j+1)], p lies in (u/2^(j+1),u/2^j], giving reciprocal sum O(1/log u) for j≤ε log₂ u with ε<1/2 by the ordinary upper bound on prime counts. The weight is O((log u/j)^δ), and summation gives O(ε^(1−δ))+o(1). Let ε tend to zero. The integrals are finite for δ<1.

Choose δ₀∈(0,1/2) so small that for all 0<δ<δ₀,

    2δ ∫_{1/K}^1 (1−t)^(−δ) dt/t < 1/2.

This is possible by dominated convergence at δ=0 with K fixed. Taking η small leaves positive slack against the initial one-half. The final quotient count is ≫_P u V(z)≫_P u/(log u)^δ, exceeding Q(u)+1 for all sufficiently large u. This proves the claim and gives the capacity criterion for all occupied bins with u large. No quantitative value of δ₀ is asserted.

The bounded-rank and growing-rank theorems in redlydi's reports cover quite different sources (all support pools with a uniform upper bound on Ω, including Ω≤0.48 log log M). For example, arbitrary powers of one fixed allowed prime have no such rank bound, while primes appearing in low-rank sources may range over all primes. A proof for the unrestricted pool would need different ideas: the quotient sieve at z=u has a parity and gap obstruction. For integer u≥3 take the interval u!+1,...,u!+u. Every offset j≥2 is divisible by a prime factor of j, so at most one position survives, while π(u)>1. This is a failure of this sufficient bin condition only.
