# Erdős–Surányi 708: corrected smooth branch and one high-prime triple branch

Status: **partial, conditional on g(7) <= 9 and g(8) <= 11 (and g(6) <= 8 for the carrier arguments inherited from earlier work).** No claim of global equality g(9)=12 is made. All computations below use exact integer feasibility conditions; floating point scores only choose the order of valid trial factors.

Let A consist of nine distinct integers >=2, M=max(A), and I be any interval of M consecutive positive integers. The full product of I is divisible by M!, which is divisible by prod(A). Bounds for smaller numbers of sources apply in I by taking a subinterval. We use the “at most” convention. An interval containing zero is trivial, and a negative interval can be reflected.

## 1. Elementary extension rules

If a selected set of <=k points supplies a requirement D, then h<=M can be appended in one new point whenever (k+1)gcd(D,h)<=M. Take a fresh multiple of h if one exists. Otherwise h is already supplied by the selected product, which is then divisible by lcm(D,h), and a fresh multiple of gcd(D,h) suffices. If the gcd is a prime, one missing prime unit can always be repaired in one point whenever Dh divides the full interval product. If no free multiple of that prime exists, the full interval valuation is already present. The gcd=1 case requires no repair. These facts prove the certificates used by the programs. They also allow a prime or 1 to be appended in a scheduled slot regardless of its size, provided the requested intermediate product divides prod(A).

Delete any two sources a,b, and cover the remaining seven by nine points. For a permutation (i,j,k) of (10,11,12), choose d|a,e|b so a/d and b/e are either primes/1 or at most M/i and M/j, respectively. If de is prime/1 or <=M/k, the three factors can be appended in their designated order. Hence the source pair cannot occur in an obstruction. The function peel uses the smallest eligible d,e for each i,j; accepting a pair is always sound. It is not required to find every possible deletion.

Similarly, in an obstruction all sources are composite and exceed M/12: delete one, cover eight by 11 points, and append the last source by a fresh multiple or a one-prime repair.

## 2. A finite cutoff for smooth pairs

For a source with all prime factors in {2,3,5,7,11} and a>=11, there is a divisor u>=11 minimal in divisibility. Such u is <=110 (remove any one prime factor), and exact enumeration gives

    11 12 14 15 16 18 20 21 25 27 35 49

For two such divisors u,v define C(u,v) as the minimum of k*d*e over permutations (i,j,k) of (10,11,12), d|u, e|v, d>=i, e>=j. The maximum of C over this finite table is 24010. The checker regenerates the table. When M>=24010, any two smooth sources in an obstruction are >=11 (since a>M/12), and this pair is removable. Thus the entirely smooth branch and any branch with at least two smooth sources need only be checked below 24010.

## 3. Complete corrected 11-smooth branch

`src/smooth_frontier.cpp` generates every smooth maximum M in [13,24009], excluding prime M. For each, let c consist of smooth composite a<M with 12a>M and a,M not removable. Repeatedly delete any a having fewer than seven other pair-incompatible members in c. Any obstruction with maximum M must contain eight members of the resulting pool, since its eight members form a clique of incompatible pairs. If fewer than eight survive, the row is empty.

The program deliberately **overenumerates**: it generates the sums of the valuations of all eight-element subsets of this pool, not only its cliques. Dynamic programming processes each distinct source once, in descending order of subset cardinality. At each step a vector can be dropped if coordinatewise dominated by another vector in the same cardinality and prefix: future additions use the same available suffix, and a cover of the dominating product covers the dominated product. The function sky computes the exact maximal vectors by an upper-orthant maximum on coordinates 1..4, with coordinate 0 stored as the maximum. Adding the maximum source M gives the demand. This covers every possible obstruction; the proof does not use a cutoff for budget 13, a restriction Omega(a)>=3, or a restriction to finitely many actual interval shifts.

For a prime p the sum of the k largest valuations in I is at least sum_{j>=1} min(k,floor(M/p^j)). Therefore independent prime covers give a certificate when the sum of the required numbers of points is <=12. After this test there are 205 nonempty maximum rows and 2,181,725 demand vectors. The largest retained maximum is 1372.

`src/filter.cpp` checks two further sufficient conditions: packing factors in bins with capacities floor(M/j), j=1,...,12; and gcd-extension sequences of at most 12 factors, including the one-prime repair. A greedy success is a proof of the corresponding product divisibility, not a sampling assertion. Every factor is chosen as a divisor of the remaining requirement. The filter accepts 196,177 by bin packing and 1,949,872 by gcd sequences. Exactly 35,676 demands remain.

`src/gcd_dp.cpp` performs exhaustive reachability of exponent vectors for gcd-extension sequences, with every factor <=M and no component above the largest required component at that M. It allows idle steps and checks gcd(D,h)<=floor(M/j). Coordinatewise dominance of a reachable terminal vector proves the demand. It closes 33,980 demands. This is an exact sufficient-certificate calculation; a failed search does not assert nonexistence of an interval cover.

`src/crt12.cpp` checks the remaining 1,696 vectors, all M<=60. For each p, truncate all valuations at the smaller of demand and floor(log_p M). This can only decrease the available supply, and the resulting row is periodic modulo the corresponding p-power. Enumerate these rows independently (a superset of all real shifts by CRT). A prefix is accepted whenever its demand has a cover with enough budget left for the unprocessed primes, using the prime layer-count lower bound and packing whole independent prime powers <=M into common points. Covers of disjoint prime sets can be united without conflict. The greedy cover checks actual valuation sums. Its exact fallback groups identical nonzero valuation vectors, respects their multiplicities, and recursively enumerates the number of points chosen from each group; memoization and remaining-coordinate supply bounds do not discard any feasible cover. Every prefix accepted is therefore sufficient; every fully enumerated profile is checked. All 1,696 demands pass.

Conclusion: **all nine-source sets using only primes 2,3,5,7,11 have tau<=12** conditional on the stated smaller-g bounds.

## 4. A normalized obstruction cannot have just one large prime of support three with p^2>M

For this section normalize a hypothetical global obstruction by minimum M and then maximum sum of its sources. It has gcd(A)=1: divide by a common divisor, use the shorter interval of quotients, and pad the cover to at least nine points before multiplying back. If quotient 1 occurs, omit it and use the eight-source bound; padding is possible since there are nine distinct positive quotients. Maximal sum forces every multiple <=M of each source to belong to A: replacing a by an absent proper multiple preserves the obstruction and increases the sum.

Assume exactly one prime p>=13 occurs, p^2>M, and its support is three. Its sources are p*u,p*v,p*w, with 2<=u<v<w<p (primes as sources have been excluded) and all cofactors smooth. The other six sources form A0 and are smooth.

For a pair of cofactors, its product h<=M. If h is not in A0, use g(7)<=9 on A0 union {h}. Choose three distinct multiples of p, including a full multiple z of the third source p*w (or the appropriate remaining source). They exist because there are at least three distinct p-source multiples in [1,M]. The p-carriers supply the full p^3 demand independently of the p-free requirement. If z is outside the old cover it also supplies its cofactor. If z is inside the old cover, its overlap saves one point, and a fresh multiple of the remaining cofactor outside the old cover restores that cofactor (at least p>=13 multiples). Hence a 12-cover exists. In an obstruction all three pair products uv,uw,vw belong to A0.

For any two cofactors, if both their squares are absent from A0, replace the source h=u*v in A0 by the two distinct sources u^2,v^2. Their product equals the required duplicate h times the deleted h, and both squares are <=M. Again use g(7) and the carrier argument. Thus at least two of the three squares are in A0. The only possible equality between the three pair products and the three squares of distinct increasing cofactors is v^2=uw.

A further condition is 10*u*v>M. Cover A0 by g(6)<=8 points and choose three p-carriers containing z divisible by p*w. If z is new, include it in the p-free cover (at most 9 points). If old, choose a fresh multiple of w outside the old cover; the overlap with the p-carriers pays for this. Then if 10*u*v<=M, a fresh multiple of uv outside this p-free cover restores the remaining pair product. Union with the three p-carriers uses at most 12 points. (This argument requires the earlier g(6)<=8 as well.)

Since u*v<=M^2/p^2, a necessary condition is p^2<10M. By Section 2, M<24010. The program `src/high3_full.cpp` enumerates p primes with p^2<240100, every increasing smooth cofactor triple with largest cofactor at most min(p-1,24009/p), and each choice of two required squares. It requires M<=min(24009,p^2-1,10uv-1). It fills the remaining one or two sources from the smooth pool, rejecting only established pair deletions or a source <=M/12, and enforcing the two normalization conditions at the leaves. It uses the smallest possible maximum of the partial set for early pair deletion; each successful pair certificate stays valid as M increases. The result is 4,970 completed enumeration leaves (duplicates allowed when three squares occur), 4,882 distinct records consisting of M,p,small demand and the three anchor cofactor vectors. Largest retained M is 960.

`src/high3check.cpp` verifies 4,855 records directly. Three arbitrary distinct p-carriers cover p^3. Independently the p-free demand can be covered by <=9 points via prime covers, bin packing, or a gcd sequence. The anchored variant chooses one of the three full-source multiples z in the p-carrier set, starts the p-free cover with its cofactor, then extends in nine further slots, giving at most 10 p-free points including z. The union thus uses <=3+9=12, with no double counting of valuations because the demands are on disjoint prime sets. The 27 remaining records give 26 distinct unanchored p-free demands; `src/high3_dp.cpp` proves 23 of these by 9-slot gcd sequences, and `src/crt9.cpp` proves the last three by complete profile enumeration (M=156, primes 2 and 3). Therefore no globally minimal, maximal-sum obstruction has this type.

## 5. What has not been proved

This package does not establish the full mixed region. In particular, the one-large-prime cases with larger support and the two-large-prime cases require further work. It also does not replay the independent smaller-g proofs; their bounds are assumptions here. The new smooth branch is independent of the previously erroneous 13-budget pruning. The high-prime triple cutoff here is 24010 and is independent of the separate claimed mixed-tail cutoff 9450. The previously obtained at-most-two-large-primes reduction is an additional result, not a consequence of this package. The global conditional range remains 12<=g(9)<=13.
