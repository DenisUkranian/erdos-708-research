# Erdős–Surányi 708: a smaller high–high frontier for n=9

**Partial result, not an exact proof of g(9).** Assume the upstream bounds g(7)<=9 and g(8)<=11. Assume a normalized global obstruction A of nine distinct integers, M=max(A), with exactly two primes >=13, both p,q>sqrt(M). Normalize by choosing minimal M, then maximal sum of A. The following computation shows M<=4361 and restricts M to 529 integer values. It does not close the remaining frontier or the other mixed branches.

## Basic lemmas

All smaller-source bounds apply inside any longer interval by choosing a subinterval. The whole interval supplies the full product P=prod(A), since P divides M! and M! divides any product of M consecutive positive integers. One missing prime unit of a required divisor of P can therefore always be added in at most one new position. A factor h can be appended freshly in step j if j*h<=M. A source <=M/12, or a prime source, cannot occur in an obstruction, by g(8)<=11. The maximal-sum normalization implies that every multiple <=M of a source must already be in A: replacement by an absent multiple preserves an obstruction and increases the sum. These facts are used only for the normalized global obstruction.

If a,b are two sources, cover the other seven in <=9 positions. If divisors d|a,e|b have a/d, b/e, de each either prime, 1, or small enough for the three respective positions (i,j,k), a permutation of (10,11,12), then these factors can be appended in <=3 steps. Their product is ab. Thus the pair is incompatible with an obstruction. In the code `peel` uses one sufficient test: choose the smallest eligible d,e for fixed i,j and check de. The table C(u,v) for singleton high sources p*u and q*v (or p*u and p*v) uses only divisors of their cofactors. If d=u, the quotient is prime; if d>=i, the quotient is at most M/i. Thus C(u,v)<=M is always a valid pair deletion.

## Support four for each high prime

The earlier support-at-least-three reduction follows from g(8), g(7), prime carriers and gcd extension. To see it directly in this high–high branch, if p divides one source p*u, the remaining eight are p-free and the gcd with p*u is <=M/p<=M/12. If p divides exactly two sources, cover seven p-free sources in nine points, choose two distinct p-multiples including one full-source multiple w. If w is old, its overlap pays for adding its cofactor; otherwise it supplies that cofactor. Add the other cofactor outside the small cover, which is possible because each cofactor has at least p>=13 multiples. Unite the covers, at most 12 points. The small demand is p-free, so sharing carrier points causes no double counting of p.

Suppose p divides exactly three sources p*u,p*v,p*w. Their distinct cofactors u<v<w are less than sqrt(M), so all three pair products and all three squares are <=M, and none contains p or q. Let A0 be the six p-free sources. If a pair product h is absent from A0, use g(7)<=9 on A0 union {h}; then choose three p-carriers including a full multiple of the remaining source. If the marked multiple is already in the old cover, its overlap pays for one fresh multiple of its cofactor. The union costs <=12. Hence all three pair products belong to A0. If two squares are absent, replace the corresponding pair product in A0 with the two squares; this gives seven distinct sources whose product equals the old product times that pair product. The same argument applies. Thus at least two squares belong to A0. At most one of these squares can coincide with a pair product, namely v^2=u*w. There must be at least four distinct sources in A0 not divisible by q. But q divides at least three of the six p-free sources, contradiction. Both p and q consequently have support >=4, with support sizes (4,4), (4,5), or (5,4). Their source groups are disjoint because p*q>M, and all their cofactors are 11-smooth integers >=2. At most one source is 11-smooth itself.

## Tail M>=24010

Any 11-smooth cofactor >=11 has a divisibility-minimal divisor >=11, no larger than 110. The exhaustive list and maximum pair threshold are independently generated in verify.cpp. For any two such minimal divisors the maximum over pairs of the minimum k*d*e (d|u,e|v,d>=i,e>=j) is 24010. So there can be at most one cofactor >=11 when M>=24010. There are at least eight cofactors, hence two <=10. Their product <=100 can be appended in one slot, and the two prime quotients can be repaired in two more slots, contradicting an obstruction. This proves the tail without using the large-M theorem from the supplied report.

## Finite generator

For every integer 13<=M<=24009, verify.cpp enumerates all high primes and their sets of four or five distinct allowed cofactors, retaining all sets that meet the necessary pair and upward-closure tests. A prime-source maximum and a maximum with a low prime >=13 cannot occur in this branch. For a smooth maximum M, the two groups have size four and the remaining source is M; each group member must be incompatible with M. For a maximum M=p*u, one group must contain the cofactor u=M/p. The generator checks cross-pair incompatibilities using the cofactor table. It intentionally overenumerates: when the maximum is not smooth and there are eight high sources, the unknown ninth smooth source is not enumerated, nor are all global normalization constraints imposed. Every actual normalized obstruction of this type is included.

For each pair of groups two additional sufficient tests are used:

1. **Three-source compression.** If three of the high sources have a cofactor product h<=M different from every remaining source, replace those three sources with h. Apply g(7)<=9, then restore the three omitted prime units individually. The only possible equality of h with a remaining source is the single smooth source, if present. If there are two distinct eligible triple products, at least one differs from it. If the smooth source is known (M), check directly. If there are nine high sources, any h<=M works. All cofactor triples are enumerated.
2. **Three-bin anchored cover.** Mark a full-source multiple of the largest cofactor in each group. If there is a smooth source, first cover it with one point. The two marked multiples give the two marked small cofactors: if a marked point is already in the small cover, append a fresh multiple of the gcd of the old small demand and that cofactor. This gcd is <=M/p or <=M/q and has >=13 multiples. Hence the small cover uses at most r=11-m positions (r=3 if m=8 high sources, r=2 if m=9), contains both marked positions, and covers the marked small cofactors and the possible smooth source. The remaining small prime factors are packed into three bins of capacities floor(M/(r+j)), j=1,2,3. Each bin product can be appended by a fresh small-cover point. Separately choose d_p distinct p-carriers and d_q distinct q-carriers containing the respective marked points. These banks exist because the respective sources are distinct <=M. The union has at least two duplicate memberships across the three sets (even if both marked points coincide); the prime demands of the banks and small cover are disjoint. Its cardinality is at most m+r+3-2=12. The three greedy packing modes accept only exact integer capacity successes. Failures are kept.

The counts are 26,897,751 group pairs before these tests, 283,065 closed by compression, 569,957 closed by packing, 26,044,729 retained. Retained pairs occur at exactly 529 M values, with maximum M=4361. The enumeration deliberately does not identify individual remaining sets as true obstructions. M<13 cannot contain a high prime >=13 in a composite source. The upstream bounds and normalization are essential dependencies.

## Replay

Run `python3 verify.py` with a C++17 compiler. It builds and runs the finite generator and checks the exact counts against the saved log. No claims are made about the rest of the mixed frontier or about an independent replay of the upstream g(7),g(8) proofs.
