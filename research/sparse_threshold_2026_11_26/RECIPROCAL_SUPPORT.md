# Exact one-atom transfer below reciprocal mass one

Let Q be any set of primes up to M, allowed to change with M. Let A be any n distinct integers from [2,M]; the ambient interval may extend past max A. Let I be any interval of M consecutive positive integers. For every fixed epsilon>0 and all sufficiently large M, if sum_{q in Q}1/q<=1-epsilon, there is a set of at most 2n-1 distinct target positions that dominates the source counts at every Q-prime-power level. In particular this proves the ordinary and level coefficient two at all densities for sources supported on Q. More generally the same statement covers the joint Q-projection of arbitrary sources, including many rows with identical Q-part and arbitrary unpriced cofactors. We assume n>=1. The theorem bounds the actual global dual at every arbitrary choice of prices on Q, because Q-level domination implies that the target product is divisible by the full projected source product.

Write a_Q for the full Q-part of a. For each row with at least two distinct Q-bases, allow candidate sites divisible by a_Q/q^{v_q(a)} for ANY of its Q-bases q. A single representative thus supplies that row's entire projected demand except at one base. We prove every nonempty subset S of these rows has at least |S|+1 neighbors. Let T be its entire neighborhood; if T=I this is automatic since |S|<=M-1. A divisor d is trapped if every multiple of d in I belongs to T. The divisor 1 is not trapped.

For each a in S use the particular candidate cofactor formed by omitting the full atom of its largest Q-base. It is trapped. Traverse the factors in that cofactor in increasing order of prime base, with multiplicities. Let d be the first trapped prefix product and let r be the last prime in d. Put H=M/d. The remaining source quotient a/d has a Q-prime q>=r, since the omitted largest base survives (in fact it exceeds r). Hence the number S_d of rows first assigned to d is bounded above by

  H sum_{q in Q, r<=q<=H} 1/q.

This counts distinct original integers a, regardless of inactive cofactors. In particular r<=H in every occupied class. On the target take every b=dv in I for which v is divisible by no prime in Q strictly below r. Each such b belongs to T, and the increasing list of its Q-primes, with multiplicity, has precisely the same first trapped prefix d. Extra copies of r in v cause no ambiguity. These target classes are disjoint across d, even when the corresponding full banks intersect. The class contains at least

  H (1-sum_{q in Q, q<r}1/q) -1-pi(r)

positions by the union bound for forbidden divisors over a quotient interval of real length H. The source and exclusion prime ranges are disjoint. Consequently the class has size >=|S_d|+epsilon H-1-pi(H). Choose a fixed H0=H0(epsilon)>=3 so that for all H>=H0 the error is <=epsilon H/2, using pi(H)=o(H). The union of regular target classes has size >=(1+epsilon/2)|S_good|.

In an exceptional occupied class H<H0 all primes of d are at most H0 and the entire remaining quotient a/d is at most H0. Consequently every Q-base of a is at most H0 and its inactive part is at most H0, while a_Q>=d>M/H0. There are at most E=ceil(H0)(1+log_2 M)^{pi(H0)} such rows in the whole canonical interval. If |S_good|>=2(E+1)/epsilon, the regular surplus pays every exceptional row plus one spare site. If there are no exceptional rows, the strictly positive regular surplus gives one spare by integrality. In the remaining case choose one exceptional row. With K=pi(H0), its numerically largest full Q-atom is at least (M/H0)^{1/K}. The neighborhood of this row includes every multiple of the cofactor formed by removing this atom (we allowed all choices); since a_Q<=M, this gives at least floor((M/H0)^{1/K}) sites. This eventually exceeds E+2(E+1)/epsilon+1, enough for the whole set with a spare. This proves Hall's surplus uniformly.

Anchor any one row at a multiple of its entire Q-part in I (if the projection is 1, choose any site). Remove that row and position. Hall now matches every remaining row with at least two Q-bases to a distinct nonanchor representative. All other unanchored rows have at most one projected full atom. Thus each unanchored row requires at most one full atom to be repaired at one base. For each q let u_q be the number of unpaid atoms at q. At any q-level its deficiency is at most u_q. Adding the u_q highest-valued fresh q-multiples, or all of them when fewer remain, repairs every level at q: all I contains at least floor(M/q^j) target hits, as many as the canonical interval. Union these repairs over q. The augmented set has size <=1+(n-1)+(n-1)=2n-1, and the desired divisibility follows.

This differs from the previous growing-support projection result with reciprocal mass below one half in [the earlier projection theorem](../twelve_routes_2026_11_26/PRICE_AND_INVERSE.md). Its exclusive banks rejected every Q-prime up to the quotient length. The first trapped prefix rejects only primes strictly smaller than its last prime; source rows still have a prime in the complementary tail. The condition is on support mass, with no restriction on the nonzero price values. Unrestricted supports can exceed mass one.

## Strengthening the physical theorem: a growing cheap-prime core is free

Set z=floor(log log log M) for large M and H0=(log M)^(1/3), natural logarithms. In the physical 2n-1 theorem it suffices that sum_{q in Q,q>z}1/q<=1-epsilon for fixed epsilon>0. No constraint is imposed on which primes up to z are in Q. The total reciprocal mass of Q may now diverge. The proof also applies to any larger z with 2^pi(z)/V=o(log H0) and z<=H0, where V=product_{q in Q,q<=z}(1-1/q).

Retain the same Hall sets, first trapped prefix d, last prime r and real length H=M/d. Put B=2^pi(z). The count in a real interval of length F of integers avoiding any subset C of primes through z differs from F times product_{q in C}(1-1/q) by at most B, by inclusion-exclusion. The same estimate on the multiples of a remote prime q>z has main term F/q times that product and error at most B, using coprimality. These estimates hold on canonical and shifted quotient intervals.

If r>z, both the source quotient and the target comparison class avoid every Q-core prime. The source quotient also has a remote Q-prime q>=r; the first-prefix factor list makes any earlier core factor in this quotient impossible. The target may be counted by deleting the multiples of every remote Q-prime below r from the core-free candidates. Since an occupied prefix has r<=H, the target size minus the source assigned count is at least V H[1-sum_{z<q<=H,q in Q}1/q]-O(B(1+pi(H))). If r<=z, the target class is its interval of quotients avoiding Q-primes strictly below r. Its canonical and target sizes differ by at most 2B. The assigned source quotients are in that canonical class, but omit all fully Q-free integers: their deleted full atom has a base larger than r. The canonical count of fully Q-free integers is at least V H[1-sum_{z<q<=H,q in Q}1/q]-O(B(1+pi(H))), by first imposing the core exclusions and then subtracting all remote Q-prime multiples. Thus the same surplus holds at every occupied d.

For H>=H0 the combined error is o(VH), since pi(H)=O(H/log H) and B/V=o(log H0). For the explicit z this last claim even follows from the elementary bounds V>=1/(floor(z)+1), by including every integer in the telescoping product, and pi(z)=o(z). Thus regular classes have surplus at least epsilon V H/2. If H<H0 the same exceptional-row count E=ceil(H0)(1+log_2 M)^pi(H0) applies; its logarithm is O((log M)^(1/3)log log M). A single such row has an alternative bank of size at least floor((M/H0)^(1/pi(H0))), whose logarithm is at least c(log M)^(2/3). This exceeds 2(E+1)/(epsilon V)+E+1 in cardinality. If regular rows are at least 2(E+1)/(epsilon V), their surplus already suffices. The matching, anchor, and final level repairs are unchanged. The statement is exact, all densities and all shifts, for sufficiently large M depending on epsilon.

### A larger free core by elementary truncated inclusion-exclusion

For any fixed 0<alpha<1/2 the core cutoff can be taken as z=exp((log H0)^alpha), H0=(log M)^(1/3), with the same physical conclusion. More generally any smaller z is covered by adding all positive-price primes up to this displayed cutoff to the core. Put L0=log H0 and fix alpha<beta<1-alpha. Let U=exp(L0^beta). For every subset C of primes through z set V_C=product_{p in C}(1-1/p). Uniformly in C and any real interval of length t, its count of C-free integers is

  t V_C+O(t/(log z)^3+B),   B=(1+z)^(k+2),

where k is an even integer between 20(log log z+10) and 20(log log z+10)+2. Indeed apply the even and odd Bonferroni truncations at k and k+1 both to the integer sieve and to the independent probability, each tuple count having floor error <=1. The tail between these two polynomial approximations on the independent space is bounded by (sum_{p<=z}1/p)^(k+1)/(k+1)! = O((log z)^(-3)) by the prime reciprocal estimate and k!>=(k/e)^k. This also proves the asserted interval count. The identical formula on multiples of a remote q applies with t replaced by t/q. Here B=exp(O(L0^alpha log L0))=o(U^(1/2)).

In either prefix comparison use this estimate on all remote q<=H/U. Their total sieve error when summed is O(H/(log z)^3 + B(1+H/U)), by the assumed bounded remote reciprocal mass. For all H/U<q<=H discard the sieve constraint entirely, bounding the actual count by H/q+1. Their aggregate cost is O(H(log U+1)/log H) uniformly for H>=H0: split [H/U,H] into dyadic bands, apply the prime counting upper bound to each band, and note log(H/U)>=(1-o(1))log H. This also bounds the sum of any discarded main terms. Finally V=V_{Q intersect [2,z]} is at least c/log z by Mertens' product estimate. All three errors are o(HV) because beta<1-alpha. This proves the same positive regular-class surplus as before. The bound on exceptional rows and on the needed inverse surplus remain valid since 1/V=O(log z). All estimates are relative to the source size only through Hall and its spare, so the final bound is still exact for every density.
