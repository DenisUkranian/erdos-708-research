# One exceptional high band through the threshold 11/9

Let $A\subseteq[1,M]$ contain $n$ distinct integers. For each fixed $\varepsilon>0$ there is a $c_\varepsilon>0$ such that, on every interval $I$ of $M$ positive consecutive integers,
$$
 M\ge n^{11/9+\varepsilon}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H(A,I)\le 2n+O_\varepsilon(n^{1-c_\varepsilon}).
$$
Here $H$ requires the selected positions to contain at least as many multiples of each prime power as the source. This implies the ordinary product requirement.

Use [the two-label construction](FIVE_QUARTERS.md) and [the exceptional-record decomposition](BLOCKED_PROGRESSIONS.md). Put $n\le M^\beta$, $G=1-\beta$ and $X=nM^\lambda$. It suffices to treat $4/5\le\beta<9/11$. All the parameter inequalities below have strict margins. Records with zero or at least two retained high bands meet the ordinary record test, since $\beta/G<9/2$. An exceptional record has only one retained high band, with lower endpoint at least $M^t$, where $t=2G-2\rho-2\xi$. Its label is $h=vpq$ with exactly two large primes $p<q$, both to exponent one, and $v\le M^{\beta+\lambda-2t}\le M^{G-c}$ for a fixed $c>0$. The previous decomposition only needs these record bounds here. We use the seven-term arithmetic projection estimate with $D=7/4$. The inequality $\beta<9/11<2D/(3D-1)=14/17$ will control the discarded labels.

**Separate ordinary, primary exceptional, and restoration windows.** Reserve two disjoint consecutive windows of length floor(M^(1−δ)) at one end of I, with δ>0 much smaller than ρ. Run the main and all good-record assignments in the remaining consecutive window exactly as in the previous proof, incurring a power-small new truncation on B lists. Subdivide the first exceptional window into a fixed number of consecutive intervals, one per low-band record of v. Only the fixed exponent bands through Y=M^(G−ρ) are needed: v≤M^(G−c)<Y. All primes ≤u=M^θ are screened, and among u<r≤Y screen every band occupied by v. The counts of bands and of masks are finite, independent of M, and each mask interval has length H0≫M^(1−δ). Different records never share a primary site. For a given h=vpq, its candidate multiples b on its mask interval have b/h free of primes ≤u or in any occupied low band. At a candidate site the complete part supported on those primes equals v, since p,q>Y. Thus two labels at the same site necessarily have identical v. No constraint on the accidental p- and q-divisibility of b/h is needed.

**Many genuine seven-term progressions in each bank.** Write K0=7!, and form P(x)=∏_{i=0}^6(x+iK0). In the h-quotient the possible starting indices have consecutive interval length H=H0/h+O(1); here H≥M^(G−δ−λ+o(1)) and deleting a constant margin at the ends preserves this bound. There are ≫ H/(log M)^7 indices x for which P(x) is free of all the screened primes. Indeed for any p≤7 there is one prohibited class mod p, and for p>7 there are seven. For squarefree d the exact count of residue classes is the product of these local counts ν_p, and the interval counting remainder is ≤7^ω(d). The dimension-seven upper and lower fundamental lemmas at sieve level H/(log M)^C thus give lower bound c H/(log u)^7 for the initial small sieve, after taking θ small so that log(level)/log u is large. To count loss from any low-band prime r>u and any tuple position i, the fixed constraint x≡−iK0 mod r gives a parameter interval of length H/r+O(1). For primes ≤u the change of variable is invertible, so the residue counts and error bounds are identical; the upper dimension-seven sieve gives at most C0 H/[r(log u)^7]. This is uniform since H/r≥M^(ρ−δ−λ+o(1)). Taking the level to be the corresponding length divided by a fixed power of log M absorbs the sum of 7^ω(d) remainder weights (and any fixed extra divisor weights). Mertens gives small primes survival product ≍(log u)^−7, with positive constant since the pattern is admissible. Choose θ sufficiently small for these two estimates, then choose the low bands narrow enough that the combined harmonic mass of at most ceil(2/θ) occupied bands is less than c/(14 C0). The union bound proves the tuple assertion. One can take the same finer bands in the good construction, which only changes constants. Every surviving x gives a seven-term progression of real candidate multiples of h with difference K0 h. This bound counts tuples globally in the mask window, without any assertion about sieve density in a bounded block.

**Primary representations.** An occurrence of h=vpq may use any candidate site with no previous registration of its largest prime q. Register q there on success, and omit the occurrence otherwise. The final set S_{v,q} of sites registered with q and screened part v has cardinality ≤X/(vq): every contributing original class C occurrence is divisible by vq, and the heavy extraction supplies this multiplicity bound. For clarity, it is the original source rows that are distinct, and all the throughputs here count occurrences of quotient labels. When a numerical h has at least one failed occurrence, all its candidate sites lie in S_{v,q}; exactness of v and uniqueness of low masks are essential here.

Fix a mask, dyadic K≤h<2K and pair (v,q). Denote by N_{v,q} the number of distinct failed values in this range. Cover the mask window by physical blocks [jB,(j+2)B) at consecutive offsets jB, B=ceil(20·7!·K), adding end blocks if needed. Every tuple of span 6·7!·h is in at least one such block. A point is in at most two blocks (a bounded constant would suffice), and a block has O(1) multiples of any h. Thus for each failed h at least c H0/[K(log M)^7] blocks contain a registered seven-term progression of difference 7! h. If a block represents m labels these differences are distinct. The Katz–Tao arithmetic projection inequality implies |S_{v,q}∩block|≫m^(4/7) by choosing endpoints x,y of one seven-term progression for each positive difference and bounding projections x,y,x+y,x+2y by dilates of the set (indices 0,6,3,4). Summing over blocks and using m^(1/D)≥m N_{v,q}^(1/D−1) gives with D=7/4

    N_{v,q} ≤ M^o(1) [XK/(H0 vq)]^D.

Each such label has multiplicity ≤X/K. Since q≥sqrt(K/v), for 1≤v≤2K we have

    Σ_v v^(−D) Σ_{q≥sqrt(K/v)} q^(−D) ≪ K^((1−D)/2) Σ_v v^(−(1+D)/2) ≪ K^((1−D)/2).

It is enough to sum over all positive integers q and v. After summing K≤X dyadically and the finite masks, total failed occurrences are

    ≤ M^o(1) (X/H0)^D X·X^((D−1)/2)
    ≤ n M^[ ( (3D−1)β/2−D ) + O_D(δ+λ)+o(1) ].

This is O(nM^−c1) because β<2D/(3D−1)=14/17. The narrower unconditional β<9/11 comes solely from accepting all records with at least two recorded high bands.

**Restoration in the second reserved interval.** At every occupied primary site credit all distinct registered primes q; among its tasks choose a full anchor having smallest q, and additionally credit its p and v there. Its p<q is smaller than every registered q, and v's prime factors are below all registered q. For the other s−1 tasks at the site send a new request d=vp to the second reserved window, one request per slot saved by cohabitation. All such d≤X/M^t≤M^(β+λ−t), and 2(β−t)<1−δ after shrinking parameters, since β<9/11<5/6. Also the number of d-requests divisible by a fixed integer w is ≤X/w: they derive from distinct original occurrences divisible by w. Place equal numerical d injectively in their full banks in the second interval; capacity exceeds X/d since its length R=M^(1−δ)≫X. Use independent uniform injections across different d. For d≠e, [d,e]≤R, so each pair of requests collides with probability O(gcd(d,e)/R). For fixed d the gcd sum against other requests is at most Σ_{w|d}w(X/w)≤Xτ(d). Thus the expected number of conflicting requests is at most nX M^o(1)/R=O(n M^−c2). Fix such an assignment; set both ends of any collision aside and give the remaining requests distinct actual sites. Restoration plus primary sites cost at most the original exceptional occurrence count. For each ordinary singleton or exceptional failed label repair missing levels by the original highest-valuation argument. Counting ≤log_2 M primes per omitted mixed task yields 2n+O(n M^−c3), with smaller c3. The proof establishes the 11/9+ε sparse level-cover range without any assumption on target shift or existence of a feasible previous cover.

The external inputs are [Katz and Tao, four-projection inequality](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9906097) and [Ford, Sieve Methods, Theorem 3.6](https://ford126.web.illinois.edu/sieve2023.pdf). For sieve level $D_0$, the total interval error is at most $\sum_{d\le D_0}7^{\omega(d)}\ll D_0(1+\log D_0)^6$ on the squarefree sieve support, by the sevenfold divisor function. Thus one may divide the relevant interval length by $(\log M)^{20}$ before applying the sieve bounds with coefficients of absolute value at most one.

A window of negative integers is reflected to a positive window by absolute value. A window containing zero has a one-position ordinary product cover. For the stronger level count including zero, any such window contains at least $\lfloor M/p^j\rfloor$ multiples of each $p^j$ and the proof's interval counts and selections apply as well, with zero divisible at every level. In exact-cofactor lists zero can simply be deleted, at cost at most one possible candidate per list. The stated theorem only needs positive windows.
