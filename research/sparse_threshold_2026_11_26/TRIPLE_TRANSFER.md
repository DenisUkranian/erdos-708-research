# Two representatives for three comparable primes and transfer to the full source

Let $J=[1,M]$, let $I$ consist of $M$ consecutive positive integers and let $H$ denote simultaneous prime-power level cover. Write $P=(M^{.259},M^{.281}]\cap\mathbb P$, $y=M^{.191}$ and $Q=\{s\in\mathbb P:s\le y\}\cup P$. A triple row is $a=hpqr$ with $h\le y$ and distinct $p<q<r$ in $P$. The two label supports $hr$ and $pq$ are disjoint.

1. Every nonempty set of $m$ triple rows has $H\le 2m-1$ for sufficiently large $M$, without any density restriction.
2. For arbitrary $A\subseteq J$, $n=|A|$, if $n\le M^{8201/10000}$ then $H(A,I)\le 2n+O(n^{1-c})$ for an absolute $c>0$. This includes $M\ge n^{50/41}$ and the slightly wider $M\ge n^{10000/8201}$. In the noncubic range the exceptional triple rows in the extraction use two representatives each. The error pays for other omitted labels and conflicts involving ordinary labels.

## Banks with an exact set of credited prime powers

For $t=hr$ or $pq$ an exact bank consists of multiples $b$ of $t$ for which $b/t$ has no prime in $Q$. Thus the entire $Q$-part of $b$ is $t$. Distinct exact types have disjoint banks. Write $L=M/t$, $z=\log_M L$ and $s=\log(.281/.259)<.08153$. In an interval of length $M(1-O(M^{-a}))$ with any fixed $a>0$, the exact bank contains at least
$$
\left(\frac{2\log(z/.191-1)}{z}-\frac{2s}{z-.281}-o(1)\right)\frac{L}{\log M}.
$$
Indeed the quotient is an interval of length $(1-o(1))L$ with a polynomially small relative length change. For the small-prime exclusions, apply the lower linear sieve at level $L/(\log M)^{10}$. Throughout $.438\le z\le.741$, its parameter lies in a compact subinterval of $(2,4)$, where the lower sieve function is $2e^\gamma\log(u-1)/u$. Mertens' product gives the first term. For each forbidden prime $l\in P$ the remaining small-prime-free multiples cost at most $(2+o(1))L/[l\log(L/l)]$. Apply the upper sieve on the quotient of length $(1-o(1))L/l$, with cutoff the smaller of $y$ and the square root of the sieve level. The upper parameter is between $2-o(1)$ and $2.53$, where the upper sieve function is $2e^\gamma/u$. The integer remainder for a divisibility condition is at most one. The elementary union bound, $\log_M(L/l)\ge z-.281$ and $\sum_{l\in P}1/l=s+o(1)$ prove the estimate uniformly. See [Tao, linear sieve](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2015/01/29/254a-supplement-5-the-linear-sieve-and-chens-theorem-optional/).

On $hr$ banks $.528\le z\le.741$; the coefficient is larger than $.87$ for large $M$. On $pq$ banks $.438\le z\le.482$ it exceeds $.028$ for large $M$. For either interval $[u,v]$ one can check this by replacing the first numerator by its value at $u$, its denominator by $v$, and the second denominator by $u-.281$.

For fixed $hr$ the source rows have distinct unordered pairs $p,q$ with $pq\le L$. Summing the prime-counting upper estimate $(1+o(1))L/(p\cdot .259\log M)$ when $L/p\ge M^{.259}$ bounds their number by $(s/.259+o(1))L/\log M<.316L/\log M$. If $L/p<M^{.259}$ there are no partners. Thus even one half of every exact $hr$ bank suffices.

For the isolated theorem use exact $hr$ banks and use $pq$ banks with exact part **only on $P$** equal to $pq$. The latter have at least $(1-s-o(1))L$ points by a union bound, including prime squares in the exclusions on $b/(pq)$; the $O(|P|)$ floor errors are $o(L)$. The source demand for this pair is at most $\sum_{r\in P}\lfloor L/r\rfloor\le(s+o(1))L$ by distinctness of the rows. The two bank families are disjoint since their $P$-parts have different numbers of bases. Take one arbitrary multiple of a full anchor row and exclude its position from every remaining assignment. The uniform surplus absorbs this deletion and the other rows receive two distinct representatives each. This proves claim 1.

## Which rows require the joint construction

Use the unitary extraction, mixed main labels and quotient labels of [the sparse construction](FIVE_QUARTERS.md). The elementary cubic case already has cost at most $2n$. Otherwise $n\le M\ll n^3$. Fix $\beta=.8201$, $G=1-\beta$, $X=nM^\lambda$ as there, with sufficiently small fixed parameters. The [two-high-band reduction](THREE_PRIME_REDUCTION.md) works with the following updated margins. For three or more recorded high bands $\beta/G<16/3$ suffices. The [one-high-band transfer](SEVEN_TERM_TRANSFER.md) needs $\beta<14/17$ and $\beta<5/6$, both strict. In the exceptional two-high-band case the three prime exponents lie between $6G-\beta-o_\eta(1)$ and $\beta-3G+o_\eta(1)$, and their product exceeds $M^{4.5G-o_\eta(1)}$. Here the errors can be made arbitrarily small by fixing the construction margins sufficiently small; the bounds with explicit $3\eta,2\eta,1.5\eta$ are in the linked reduction. The limiting endpoints are $.2593,.2804$ and $1-4.5G=.19045$. Thus these are triple rows in the stated band. The three primes belong to the original quotient label. Replace BOTH labels of each such row by $hr,pq$, including the original extraction divisor in $h$. Keep every other row's labels. The pair demand of the replaced rows is at most $X/(pq)$ by the original quotient multiplicity bound (1), since each original quotient contains $pq$. This is $o(L/\log M)$ here. Consequently half of the **exact $Q$-bank** for $pq$ now suffices as well. This stronger choice is essential in the mixed theorem.

Use polynomially short disjoint consecutive subintervals for the other quotient placements and restorations. More precisely, reserve the two windows of the one-band proof and a third of length $\lfloor M^{1-a}\rfloor$ for the good records, with $a>0$ small compared with all fixed exponent margins. The two windows work as in that proof. For the third, all the good-record list estimates in sections 2–4 of the main construction apply with the same bands and labels: the quotient lengths are now at least $M^{G-\lambda-a+o(1)}$, the high-band floor error fraction is $O(M^{-2\rho+\lambda+a})$, and the smallest colored expected list still exceeds $M^c X/t$ if $a+\kappa+\lambda<\xi$. Choose $a$ also smaller than $\rho-\lambda$ and choose the tiny sieve cutoff after these choices. The remaining interval $I'$ has length $M-O(M^{1-a})$. Ordinary singleton and final deficient-level repairs use the original full $I$.

## Allowing ordinary labels on already occupied positions

In $I'$ independently mark positions with probability $1/2$. Chernoff bounds give, simultaneously, $(1/2+o(1))$ marked positions in every needed triple bank as a fraction of its size and at least one third unmarked in every ordinary exact list $V_t=\{b\in I':t\mid b,\ (t,b/t)=1\}$ used below. There are polynomially many lists, each of polynomial size. For ordinary lists $t\le M^{2/3}$ and inclusion-exclusion gives size $(M/t)M^{-o(1)}$ or greater. Place the special triples injectively in their marked exact banks. Call these positions $E$ and credit their prescribed $Q$-parts only. For any $t>1$ supported on $Q$,
$$
 |E\cap V_t|\le \#\{\text{replaced source rows }a:t\mid a,\ (t,a/t)=1\}.
$$
Indeed the exact $Q$-part at a chosen site is one label of its original row. Its two labels have disjoint supports. Thus each hit supplies an actual source row with the same exponents at the primes of $t$, once at most. This comparison concerns simultaneous source capacity and does not assume subadditivity of product cover.

For each remaining mixed main occurrence $k$ put $t=k_Q$, the full $Q$-part. For $k\ne t$ preassign a representative in $V_k$ by a uniform injection per equal $k$. Independently preassign a fallback for **every** occurrence with $t>1$, including $k=t$, by a uniform injection per equal $t$ into $V_t\setminus E$. The full and fallback families are independent conditional on $E$; different numerical values within either family use independent injections. The full lists are large. The fallback lists retain at least their unmarked third.

Trim any demand above its available list size. In a full list every requested occurrence comes from a different unitary multiple in $J$. For each fallback value $t$, each source row requesting it is a unitary multiple of $t$ and gives it only once, since the two original labels of a row are coprime. These rows are disjoint from the counted replaced source rows. Adding the preceding bound for $|E\cap V_t|$ therefore gives at most the canonical unitary count. Comparing the two intervals by inclusion-exclusion, the shortfall in either list is at most $O(M^{-a}M/t+2^{\omega(t)})$ at the corresponding value. Each overflowing list has demand at least $(M/t)M^{-o(1)}$, so over all values only $O(nM^{-c})$ tasks are lost. Discard an original task if either of its required preassignments was trimmed.

If $k=t$, use its fallback. If $k\ne t$ and its full representative lies outside $E$, use its full representative. If its full representative lies in $E$, credit $k/t$ there, and use its fallback for $t$ when $t>1$. These credits coexist with those of the triple because $k/t$ has no prime in $Q$. In all cases an ordinary task entails at most one additional paid position.

## Collisions of ordinary preassignments

It suffices to discard both tasks of every pair of preassignments of distinct tasks at a common site sharing a credited prime, even inspecting unused candidates. Equal labels in one family have distinct representatives; a full-list value cannot equal a fallback value since only $k\ne k_Q$ has a full preassignment. For compatible distinct placed values $u,v$ their gcd $g>1$ is unitary in BOTH original main labels: placements are unitary sublabels and exact lists force full agreement on common prime exponents. The greedy block inequality implies $u^2,v^2\le Mq$ for a shared full atom $q$, so $[u,v]\le M$. Independent marginal assignments then collide with probability at most $M^{o(1)}g/M$ using their list lower bounds.

For distinct original main values the sum of these common unitary divisors over pairs is $O(n W M^{o(1)})$, $W=M^{1-\lambda}$. Here is the detail needed when the original labels themselves are incompatible. Order the main groups by extraction and then the terminal labels. For an earlier group value $d$, a common unitary divisor $g$ is proper in $d$, since otherwise the later row was already extracted. It belonged to the eligible divisor family and $g r(g)<W$ by minimality at that step. For terminal pairs the same bound holds at termination. Each original value has $M^{o(1)}$ divisors and each later source row contributes at most four preassignments. Summing proves the bound, without requiring equality of exponents at primes outside the projected gcd.

For two occurrences with the same original value $k$, the only remaining collision comparison is full versus fallback. Its gcd is $t=k_Q$. The original multiplicity is at most $M/k$, and $k/t$ has a prime exceeding $y$. The total expected conflicts of this kind are at most $O(n M^{o(1)}/y)$. The expected conflicts from distinct originals are $O(nM^{-\lambda+o(1)})$. Some assignment therefore loses only $O(nM^{-c})$ more tasks. Different surviving ordinary tasks can share a position only with disjoint credited supports. Full and fallback of a single split task are in $E$ and outside $E$ respectively.

Finally every omitted mixed task costs at most $\log_2 M$ individual prime-base repairs; for each prime choose the largest remaining valuations needed to meet the maximum shortfall over its nested levels. The entire interval dominates the canonical source counts at every level. All charged positions from the three short windows and the long window are disjoint except for the explicitly shared, separately credited uses above. This proves claim 2, with a smaller $c>0$ if necessary.
