# Arithmetic projection bound for the remaining two large primes

Let $D_*$ be the largest root of $x^3-4x+2=0$, and put
$$
 e_*=(4D_*-3)/(3D_*-2)=1.2231547958290598\ldots.
$$
For every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, if $M\ge n^{e_*+\varepsilon}$, then any $n$ distinct numbers in $[1,M]$ have a prime-power level cover on every positive length-$M$ interval with at most $2n+O_\varepsilon(n^{1-c_\varepsilon})$ positions, for some $c_\varepsilon>0$. The ordinary product follows. The shorter argument with arithmetic exponent $D=7/4$ already gives $e=16/13$.

Start with the heavy-group construction, labels, and bands of [the two-label construction](FIVE_QUARTERS.md). Treat $M\ge(2n)^3$ by its elementary bound. Otherwise use $n\le M^\beta$ for a fixed $4/5\le\beta<(3D_*-2)/(4D_*-3)$, write $G=1-\beta$, and $X=nM^\lambda$. Smaller fixed $\beta$ were already covered. Choose $D>D_*$ close enough to preserve $\beta<(3D-2)/(4D-3)$. The parameter $D$ is the exponent in the projection lemma below; a slightly smaller intermediate exponent may be used to absorb arbitrary small losses in the published notation. In the simpler version one uses Katz–Tao's exact $7/4$ projection inequality.

**Exceptional records.** Fix small constants $\rho,\xi>0$; next take $\lambda>0$ sufficiently small. A record is good when its old weight $t_S\ge M^{-G+\xi}$. At zero or at least two high bands the proof in §3 of the 5/4 note makes every record good: the needed bound for $k\ge2$ is $\beta/G<\min_{k\ge2}(k+1)^2/k=9/2$, and the limiting ratio here is $4.4811943\ldots<9/2$. Equivalently this strict margin follows from $D_*>5/3$. The finitely many $k$ allow fixed small $\rho,\xi,\lambda$. For an exceptional record there is just one recorded high band, with $B_0/P<M^{-G+\xi}$. Its endpoint satisfies $P\ge M^t$ for $t=2G-2\rho-2\xi$, and the band construction gives two distinct prime divisors at least $P$, including a possible tie in the largest occupied band. Since $3t>\beta+\lambda$, each such prime occurs to exponent one and there are precisely two. Thus every exceptional label has the form
$$
 h=v p q,\quad p\ne q,\quad p,q\ge M^t,\quad v\le M^{\beta+\lambda-2t}\le M^{G-c_0}
$$
for some $c_0>0$ because $\beta<5/6$. No prime of any such $v$ is one of these large primes at any label. From the extraction, the total C multiplicity divisible by any positive integer $u$ is at most $X/u$.

**A consecutive subwindow.** Reserve the last $L_0=\lfloor M^{1-\delta}\rfloor$ consecutive positions for exceptional records, with $\delta>0$ sufficiently small. The other positions form one consecutive window of length $M-L_0$; perform all main placements and good placements/restorations there using the original construction. None of its good-list arguments changes since all quotient lengths are multiplied by $1-o(1)$. For the main list of a value $h$, inclusion–exclusion gives cardinality $((M-L_0)/h)\prod_{p\mid h}(1-1/p)+O(2^{\omega(h)})$ before coloring, while its source multiplicity is at most $(M/h)\prod_{p\mid h}(1-1/p)+O(2^{\omega(h)})$. Thus an overflowing value loses at most $O((M^{-\delta}+M^{-\kappa})\mu_B(h)+2^{\omega(h)})$ after coloring. The original main error remains a power saving.

Process the exceptional occurrences in any order. For $h=v p q$ try every multiple of $h$ in the reserved window. Place at a site at which neither $p$ nor $q$ has been used previously as one of the two large primes at that site. Register $p,q$ there on success and omit the occurrence otherwise. Let $S_u$ denote the final set of reserved sites registered with $u$; then $|S_u|\le X/u$. For a failed h all its reserved multiples belong to $S_p\cup S_q$.

**Finite arithmetic projection lemma.** For the fixed $D>D_*$ there is a fixed integer $k$ such that the number of positive differences of $k$-term progressions contained in any finite set $T\subset\mathbb Z$ is $O_D(|T|^D)$. Katz–Tao, *New bounds on Kakeya problems*, Definition 3.1 and §4, give finitely many rational projective slopes $r\ne-1$ bounding the cardinality of differences $a-b$ on a finite graph by the $D$-power of the largest projected cardinality under $a+rb$ (or $b$ at infinity). For clarity, their graph must be parameterized by $a-b$; the following graph does so. Choose one progression $x,x+d,\ldots,x+(k-1)d$ for each distinct positive $d$. With integers $m,C$ such that all the fixed $m+Cr/(1+r)$ and $m,m+C$ lie in $[0,k-1]\cap\mathbb Z$, use endpoints $a=x+md,b=x+(m+C)d$. Positive $C$ clearing the finitely many denominators, followed by a suitable shift $m$ and sufficiently large $k$, works. Then $a-b=-Cd$, every projection is a fixed dilation of a member of $T$, and the infinity projection is in $T$. This proves the lemma. For $D=7/4$, their earlier *Bounds on arithmetic projections* Theorem 1.1 only asks for projections $a,b,a+b,a+2b$, so $k=7$, using positions 0,6,3,4 in a 7-term progression.

**Failure count.** In each dyadic label range $K\le h<2K$ partition the reserved physical interval into consecutive full blocks of length $\lceil 3QK\rceil$, discarding a final remainder. Here $Q=W(2,k)$ is the finite van der Waerden number for two colors and k terms. There are $\gg_D L_0/K$ blocks, since $K\le X\le M^{\beta+\lambda}\ll L_0$. For each distinct failed $h=vpq$, every block contains at least $Q$ consecutive multiples of $h$. Color each of them according to an available blocking resource $p$ or $q$ (ties arbitrary). The van der Waerden theorem gives a $k$-term progression in one of $S_p$ or $S_q$ within the block, with positive common difference $j h$, $j\le4Q$. Thus one of $p,q$, denoted by $u(h)$, represents this $h$ in at least half of the blocks. Choices depend on the size range; all blocked sites used for this reasoning are genuine members of the eventual registered sets, including when a numerical label had several failed occurrences.

For a fixed $u$, let $N_u$ be the number of distinct failed values assigned to it in this range. If a block represents $m_i$ such values, it has at least $m_i/(4Q)$ distinct progression differences (for fixed difference, $h$ is determined by $j$). The finite projection lemma gives $|S_u\cap\text{block }i|\gg_D m_i^{1/D}$. Since $\sum_i m_i\gg_D(L_0/K)N_u$ and $m_i^{1/D}\ge m_i N_u^{1/D-1}$ for $N_u>0$,
$$
 N_u\ll_D\left(\frac{XK}{L_0 u}\right)^D .
$$
Weight each distinct failed label by its full multiplicity $\le X/K$. Summing over the integers $u\ge M^t$ and over all dyadic $K\le X$ gives at most
$$
 M^{o(1)}(X^2/L_0)^D M^{-t(D-1)}
 \le n M^{(2D-1)\beta-D-t(D-1)+2D\lambda+D\delta+o(1)}.
$$
At $t=2G$, the main exponent is $(4D-3)\beta-(3D-2)<0$. Choose $\rho,\xi,\lambda,\delta$ sufficiently small within the earlier strict conditions. The total number of failed exceptional occurrences is $O(nM^{-c_1})$ for a fixed $c_1>0$.

**Repair.** At a reserved site with $s$ successes credit its $2s$ distinct large primes, together with one of the small cofactors $v$. After the successful main and good-record placements and restorations have all been fixed, assign each of the remaining $s-1$ small cofactors at most one fresh multiple in the whole target interval. There are $\gg n$ such multiples for every $v$, and all paid allocations up to this point require at most $2n$, so this restores every deleted cofactor's full level credits. Deferred singletons, failed exceptional tasks, and main exceptions are handled by the original descending-valuation level repair at cost one per singleton or at most $\log_2 M$ per failed mixed label. This yields the stated polynomial saving, uniformly in shifts. A full cover at higher densities remains a separate problem.

Primary sources: Katz–Tao, https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0102135 (Definition 3.1, Theorem 4.1 and its iteration); their survey also explicitly states the root and SD bound at https://dmle.icmat.es/pdf/PUBLICACIONSMATEMATIQUES_2002_46_EX_07.pdf . An independent modern formulation explicitly defining the rational-slope exponents and recording the finite-slope infimum bound is Terence Tao, *Sum-difference exponents for boundedly many slopes, and rational complexity*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15135 . The shorter 7/4 theorem is https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9906097 .
