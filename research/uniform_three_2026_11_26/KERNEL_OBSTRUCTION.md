# A full-period divisor kernel can be maximally misleading at the canonical shift

Write P=lcm(1,...,M), e_p=floor(log_p M), h=sum_{d<=M}1/d, and mu(d)=1/(dh). A full-period divisor kernel is a probability rule K selecting d<=M dividing b, depending only on gcd(b,P), with output marginal mu for a uniform residue b mod P. The empirical output law w_x averages K over the window x+1,...,x+M. The previous simultaneous full-period bound used TV(mu,w_x) as an upper bound on every adaptive cut excess.

**Proposition.** For each M one can choose a full-period divisor kernel with TV(mu,w_0)=1-o(1) as M grows. The actual harmonic cut excess at x=0 is O(1/log M), and the identity map from sources to targets is an exact product cover using n positions.

Take independent geometric exponents G_p with Pr(G_p>=j)=p^(-j) for primes through M. Put V=product p^G_p and Z=gcd(V,P). The Chinese remainder theorem identifies the distribution of Z with the gcd of a uniform residue modulo P. Given V<=M its law is mu, since every such v has unconditional mass proportional to 1/v.

On V<=M set D=V. On V>M couple Z to D~mu so that D divides Z. Indeed, for any set S of candidate d's the event E that some member of S divides V is increasing, as is V>M; its conditional probability given V>M is at least its unconditional probability by Harris's inequality. This last probability is at least its conditional probability given the decreasing event V<=M, hence at least mu(S). Divisibility by a member of S is unaffected by capping V to Z. These are exactly the finite weighted Hall inequalities for the desired conditional coupling. Sample D on V>M using this coupling conditional on Z, and let K be the resulting conditional law of D given Z, across both cases. Both components have D~mu; D divides Z always.

At the canonical position b<=M, K assigns at least Pr(V=b|Z=b) to output b. This conditional probability equals the product of (1-1/p) over primes p with p^e_p dividing b, by geometric memorylessness and independence. Consequently the mean probability of changing b is at most

    (1/M) sum_{b<=M} sum_{p^e_p|b} 1/p <= sum_{p<=M} p^(-e_p-1) <= pi(M)/M = o(1).

Thus w_0 is within o(1) in TV of the uniform law on [1,M]. The set d<=M/log M has uniform mass o(1) but harmonic mass 1-o(1). TV(mu,w_0) tends to its maximum value one.

In contrast, on the window [1,M] choose U uniformly. Delete one prime p from U sampled with probability v_p(U) log(p)/log U (and send 1 to 1). Its output divides U and has marginal O(1/log M) in TV from mu by the [harmonic deletion lemma](../cofactor_flow_2026_11_26/HARMONIC_REDUCTION.md). Hence every harmonic cut has excess O(1/log M) at this very shift. More explicitly, when all the multiples of an output divisor lie in a target set T, the original U lies in T. The full-period kernel's TV majorant and the harmonic cut therefore have genuinely different worst-shift behavior. The construction does not exclude a specially selected kernel with a much stronger uniform bound.

A partial repair is possible while preserving the full-period mean theorem: replace the kernel on gcd profiles z in (M/log M,M] by the prime-deletion rule for z. This yields canonical output within O(1/log M) of mu because at most O(M/log M) canonical positions lie below the replaced range. The altered kernel's full-period marginal is within o(1) of mu: Pr(Z=z)=(1/z) product_{p: p^e_p does not divide z}(1-1/p). For z in that range the product is at most O(1/log M) times product_{p^e_p|z}(1-1/p)^(-1)=O(1) times O(1/log M). For the uniform factor note that at most one p>sqrt M can divide z, and two distinct capped powers of any primes cannot both divide z since each exceeds sqrt M except possibly a power corresponding to a prime near sqrt M, which also exceeds sqrt M: for p<=sqrt M use p^e_p>M/p>=sqrt M. Hence the product of inverse exceptions is at most two. The total full-period mass of the modified range is O(log log M/log M). The modified rule still admits the previous harmonic comparison for almost all shifts by the same Fourier variance bound (or simply by applying Markov to the empirical frequency of edited profiles), and handles this constructed bad shift. It has approximate, rather than exact, harmonic marginal.
