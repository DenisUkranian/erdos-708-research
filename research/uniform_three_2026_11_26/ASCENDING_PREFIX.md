# A prime-prefix criterion at arbitrary shifts

Let A consist of n distinct integers in [2,M], with maximum M, and let I be any positive interval of M consecutive integers. An atom is the full power of one prime in a source. In each row delete its largest numerical atom q, and write d=a/q and u=M/d. Put P⁺(1)=1 and r(d)=#{a:a/q=d}. Let H be the stronger minimum cardinality satisfying every prime-power multiplicity constraint.

**Theorem.** Fix 0<g<1/(1+sqrt(e)). If P⁺(d)≤u^g for every nonunit cofactor d, then H(A,I)≤2n−1 for all sufficiently large M, uniformly in n and the shift. It suffices that every d≤M^b for some fixed b<1/(2+sqrt(e)). There is no bound on rank, prime multiplicities or the number of primes in the source. The nonunit-test rows may coexist with arbitrary rank-one and rank-two rows, which can instead be repaired at their two-positions-per-row cost.

**Disjoint bins.** Process the prime factors of a positive target in increasing order with repetitions. Start at t=1 and append the current prime p exactly while p≤(M/(tp))^g. Freeze upon the first failure. The final t is the bin containing that target. If v has no prime divisor ≤u^g, then b=dv lies in bin d. All factors of d precede the factors coming from v and are admissible since along the prefix tp≤d and p≤P⁺(d)≤u^g. The next prime, if present, is >u^g and fails the test. Thus different d give disjoint banks. The quotients v for b∈I range through an interval of real length u.

**Capacity.** The standard one-dimensional lower linear sieve for an interval, at level D=u^(1−h), supplies at least

    [2 log((1−h)/g−1)/(1−h) + o(1)] u/log u

positions with v free of primes ≤u^g, when 2<(1−h)/g<4 and h>0 is fixed. Indeed every squarefree divisibility count differs from u divided by its modulus by at most one, and summing the level-D remainder gives O(D times a fixed power of log D)=o(u/log u). With s=(1−h)/g use the lower sieve function f(s)=2 exp(EulerGamma) log(s−1)/s and Mertens' product. All estimates are uniform in the starting position. For g below the stated threshold we may enlarge it to a fixed g' in (1/3,1/(1+sqrt(e))) with g'≥g, using g' as both the upper bound on P⁺(d) and the bin/sieve parameter. For g in this range, choose h>0 sufficiently small that the displayed leading factor exceeds 1. At most π(u)+O(sqrt(u)log(2u)) rows share d, since the deleted atom is a prime power and different rows give different q. The prime number theorem now gives capacity at least r(d)+1 for large u. The lower bound for u is uniform as M grows: if u≤U, each atom of a is at most q≤U, so d divides lcm(1,...,floor U) and M=ud≤U lcm(1,...,floor U).

Anchor any tested row at a genuine multiple of that row and reserve that position. Match every remaining nonunit cofactor inside its own bin, avoiding the anchor. Each matched row leaves at most one unpaid atom. The greatest remaining valuations of each prime repair all its nested levels at a cost of at most one position per unpaid atom: the full interval contains the required number of multiples at each level. Unit cofactors need only their singleton repair. The anchored row costs one, and each remaining row costs at most two. If low-rank rows bypassed the test at the outset, include their atoms in this same repair; if all rows bypass the test, anchor any one of them. The sufficient bound d≤M^b follows from b/(1−b)≤g after appropriate fixed choices.

**A weaker filling assumption allows larger prime factors.** The same proof works for 1/3≤g<1/2 and r(d)≤(2 log(1/g−1)−epsilon)u/log u for a fixed epsilon>0 and each occupied nonunit d. Choose h small to preserve a strict margin. This form does not require the harmonic mass of the entire source support to be small. For fixed b<1/(2+sqrt(e)) with b>0 the simpler condition includes, for example, all a≤M having a prime p>M^(1−b). There is at most one such prime per row and the largest atom is at least p. Their number is [−log(1−b)+o(1)]M by summing floor(M/p) and Mertens' estimate.

The reciprocal-blocker criterion in the preceding project update provides another way to certify distinct choices. The ordered bins here use the actual largest prime of the cofactor and the sieve lower bound. The general case may have P⁺(d) close to u and nearly as many rows as prime powers up to u; the lower linear sieve above gives no positive main term at g=1/2.
