# Two spare prime factors and the lower linear sieve

Let J=[1,M] and let I be any interval of M consecutive positive integers. For prices 0≤w_p≤1 put X(b)=Σ_{p≤M}w_p v_p(b) and E_J,E_I for interval averages. Fix 0<c≤1. Suppose w_p≤c except on primes up to some y₀=M^{o(1)}, where prices may be arbitrary. Then, uniformly over such sequences and all shifts,

    E_I min(X,1) ≤ E_J min(X,1+2c) + o(1).

Taking c=1 gives τ(A,I)≤3|A|+o(M) for all A⊆[2,M] by the boxed valuation dual and vertex rounding. Taking c=1/2 excludes these prices from producing a fixed positive-density violation of the coefficient two. A price condition at c=1/2 does not constrain every possible maximizing dual vector.

## Rough composite counts

Write Ω for the number of prime factors with multiplicity, and ω_B, f, F for Buchstab's function and the lower and upper linear sieve functions normalized by the Mertens product. For each fixed j≥3, suppose r≥M^(1/j), H≤M and H≥r². Uniformly over real quotient intervals of length H the number of integers with all prime factors greater than r is at least

    #{m≤H : every prime factor of m is >r, Ω(m)≥2} − o_j(H/log r).

Indeed let v=log H/log r∈[2,j]. The canonical rough count is (ω_B(v)+o_j(1))H/log r by the Buchstab estimate, uniformly on this compact range. Removing primes and the integer one subtracts (1/v+o_j(1))H/log r, by the prime number theorem and H≥r². On the shifted interval the dimension-one linear sieve gives the lower count (exp(−γ) f(v)−o_j(1))H/log r. Use level D=H/(log H)^C for fixed sufficiently large C, each divisibility remainder bounded by one, Mertens' product and uniform continuity of f on compact intervals. One has exp(−γ)f(v)≥ω_B(v)−1/v for v≥2. Indeed v(ω_B(v)−exp(−γ)f(v)) equals one at v=2, and its derivative for v>2 equals ω_B(v−1)−exp(−γ)F(v−1)≤0, by F+f=2 exp(γ)ω_B and F≥f. This includes v=2 by continuity. See [Tao, Theorem 2 and the differential equations of the linear sieve](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2015/01/29/254a-supplement-5-the-linear-sieve-and-chens-theorem-optional/) and [Vaughan, Chapter 7, (42)](https://personal.science.psu.edu/rcv4/568s20/568chapter7.pdf).

## Comparing the cap increments

Fix a sufficiently large integer j and put Y=M^(1/j). For large M this cutoff contains all the exceptional prices. Write s for the exact load from p≤Y. For the other primes use only their distinct occurrence indicators; discarding repeated occurrences changes bounded capped averages by o_j(1), since the proportion divisible by p² for some p>Y is at most Σ_{p>Y}p^(−2)+π(M)/M. Denote the distinct-prime load by W.

Handle s≥1 with target cap equal to one and canonical cap at least one. For s<1 cancel the core load s and put h=1−s. The remaining caps are min(W,h) and min(W,h+2c). Write these as W minus their positive-part hinges. The common core term min(s,1) and the linear means W·1_{s<1} transfer between the intervals with an error tending to zero as first M→∞ then j→∞. Details are given below.

Order the primes greater than Y by size. On adding prime q the hinge increment is its occurrence indicator times the integral over 0<z<w_q of the indicator that the strictly earlier load exceeds the relevant threshold minus z. Endpoints of integration do not matter. Write u=h−z. For u<0 the target indicator is one, hence its integral dominates the source after the same single-prime core transfer. Consider u≥0. A canonical row counted at q has earlier load exceeding u+2c. Even after removing the two largest present earlier *positive-price* primes, the remaining load still exceeds u. Its shortest ascending prefix B of present positive-price earlier primes crossing u therefore leaves two distinct further primes, each larger than r=max B and smaller than q. Put d=q∏_{p∈B}p.

For a fixed B split each quotient of a d-multiple into w m, with w its full part supported on all primes ≤r. Every counted canonical quotient in the B-class yields a composite m with all prime factors >r. Only w for which H=M/(dw)≥r² need be included. For given d,w the core load s is fixed by w since d and m contain no primes ≤Y. Also w contains none of the positive-price primes <r outside B, by the prefix property. Conversely, a target d w m with the same w and r-rough m has this same first crossing B whenever (s,z) meets the prefix eligibility conditions. As m varies its allowed target range is an interval of real length H. These target sets for fixed q,z are disjoint over the eligible B,w: the shortest present prefix and the smooth part of the quotient are uniquely determined. The rough-composite estimate thus compares each counted source class from above to its target class, up to o_j(H/log r). For each d, summing over w costs at most (M/d)(1+log M)/log r=O_j(M/d) times the uniform o_j(1). There are at most j primes in d and q is its largest. The reciprocal sum over all such d is at most ∏_{Y<p≤M}(1+1/p)=O_j(1). The z integrals have length at most one and may be interchanged with these finite sums. The total normalized error at fixed j tends to zero.

For the core comparisons use the [uniform valuation-profile bound](../unrestricted_shifts_2026_11_26/SUBPOWER_PROFILES.md). At any quotient interval of length at least M^(1/sqrt(j))/2, the law of exponents through Y is within ε_j=O(exp(−c₀ sqrt(j) log j)) of the common independent geometric model, for an absolute c₀>0 and large M (first fix j). On q-multiples this core is precisely the core of the quotient. Hence the bare bounded core term transfers. All the single-prime sums, including the integrated u<0 terms, have bounded coefficients that depend only on that core. For q≤M^(1−1/sqrt(j)) use the comparison with total error O(ε_j (1+log j))+o_j(1), since Σ_{Y<q≤M}1/q=O(1+log j) and the endpoint-count errors cost O(π(M)/M). For larger q the total weight is O(1/sqrt(j))+o_j(1) by Mertens. Sending j to infinity proves the cap inequality.

For the covering consequence, each level of every prime in I has total count at least that on J, hence Σ_I X≥Σ_J X. For the dual objective Φ=Σ_A X−Σ_I(X−1)_+ one obtains Φ≤Σ_I min(X,1)−Σ_{J outside A}X≤(1+2c)|A|+o(M) at every vector satisfying the hypotheses. At c=1 the bound holds for the maximum; standard LP vertex rounding adds at most π(M)=o(M).
