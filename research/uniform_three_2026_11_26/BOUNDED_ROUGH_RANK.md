# Bounded rough rank above a stretched logarithmic cutoff

Fix a positive integer R and 0<β<1/2. For every source a suppose a full-atom divisor h can be chosen such that, writing X=M/h and z=exp((log X)^β), all primes of h are at most z, while a/h has at most R distinct prime bases and all of them exceed z. Then H≤(2+o(1))n uniformly in all source densities and shifts. This gives unrestricted prime multiplicities in both parts. No square-root cofactor hypothesis is imposed. As usual, when X is bounded, read the expression for z literally. The source interval is [2,M], and H denotes the simultaneous prime-power-level cover.

For any chosen core h, each target b=h m, with no p≤z dividing m, belongs to a bin specific to h. Process prime factors p of b in increasing order with repetitions, starting at t=1 and appending p while tp≤M and p≤exp((log(M/(tp)))^β). All factors of h pass, and the next one fails, because the cutoff decreases with the running product. This defines disjoint banks for all cores. Work in the length-X quotient interval for a fixed h.

Split the full prime-power atoms of a/h in descending numerical order into two blocks by adding the next atom to the smaller running product. Designate the smaller final block v as the first label (including 1), the other as the second. All first labels will be given distinct representatives, paying the h-core by a separate position per source. Each nonunit block w satisfies w²≤X q for every atom q of w: its last-added atom q₀ was the smallest and immediately before it the other block was ≥w/q₀. Any block with at least two atoms thus has w≤X^(2/3), since q₀²≤w. First labels always have v≤sqrt(X). Defer a second label of exactly one atom for nested-level repair. Ignore unit second labels. Generate a task for every first label and for every second label with at least two atoms.

The list for a task labeled w consists of the multiples of w in the quotient interval whose remaining factor has no prime ≤z and is coprime to w. Every listed target therefore has the exact required valuation at every base of w. For large X, there are ≫ X/(w log z) choices uniformly: the quotient length is T=X/w≥X^(1/3), and sieving through z=T^o(1) gives ≫T/log z positions by the one-dimensional fundamental lemma and Mertens' product. Forbidding an extra factor of each prime dividing w removes at most R(T/z+1), negligible in comparison.

Choose independent uniform representatives, one per task. Forbid coincidences between any two first tasks, and between any two tasks sharing a prime base. Assignments from different h are already disjoint. If two labels have different required valuations at a shared prime their lists are disjoint. In every remaining forbidden pair w,w', lcm(w,w')≤X. For two first labels this follows from the square-root bounds. For a shared prime p with matched exponent e, the balanced inequalities w²,(w')²≤X p^e imply ww'/p^e≤X. Thus each forbidden coincidence has probability ≪ gcd(w,w')(log z)^2/X, by counting interval multiples of their lcm. This applies to the first label 1 as well. The gcd in every pair with nonempty intersection is a full-atom divisor of w.

For U≥3 write F_R(U)=#{m≤U:ω(m)≤R}. Then F_R(U)≪_R U(1+log log U)^R/log U. Indeed discard integers ≤sqrt(U). The others have an atom q≥U^(1/(2R)); fix the complementary m and count prime powers q≤U/m by O_R(U/(m log U)). This uses the prime counting upper bound and O(sqrt(t)log(2t)) for higher powers. Sum over m with at most R−1 prime bases; the reciprocal sum is O_R((1+log log U)^(R−1)) by expanding products of Σ_{p≤U}1/(p−1).

For fixed w and any full-atom divisor t|w, there are ≤2 F_R(X/t) tasks whose labels contain t, since each distinct quotient source contributes at most two tasks. Include t=1. Summing the collision probabilities for one fixed task over its conflicting partners therefore gives at most

    O_R( (log z)^2 / X ) Σ_{t full-atom divisor of w} t F_R(X/t)
       ≪_R (log X)^(2β−1)(1+log log X)^R = o(1).

There are at most 2^R summands, and X/t≥X^(1/3). The asymmetric Lovász local lemma applies: take x_E=2Pr(E) for each nonzero bad event. For X large each task has incident probability sum ≤1/8, so the sum of x over the neighbors of an event (sharing either of its tasks) is at most 1/2. The product of 1−x over them is at least 1/2, proving the local lemma condition.

The resulting choices pay the h-core at distinct first representatives and every assigned outside atom at a distinct representative for its base. The representatives' quotients have no core primes. Positions coinciding between different tasks therefore keep all their credited prime-power counts. The missing singleton second labels are restored by nested level repair, costing at most one additional position apiece. The overall budget is two positions per row. Bounded X≤X₀(R,β) contributes at most polylog(M) rows in total because their h are supported on a fixed finite prime set and a/h is bounded. Their possible atom cost is polylog(M) and hence o(n) when M<(2n)^3. Otherwise the unconditional two-block bound is already sufficient.

This uses the balanced-block Lovász local lemma method of [redlydi, bounded prime factors](https://github.com/redlydi/Erdos708-Research/blob/32f397f8f/reports/bounded-prime-factors.md). The choice of first representatives, disjoint variable-core banks, and use of full-atom divisors of compatible labels allow the present cutoff and arbitrary multiplicities. For the standard sieve input see [Ford, Sieve Methods Lecture Notes](https://ford126.web.illinois.edu/sieve2023.pdf).
