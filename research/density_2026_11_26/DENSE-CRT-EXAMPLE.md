# A dense CRT counterexample to uniform n+o(n), not to 2n

Let S contain all primes in (M^(1/3),sqrt M], let λ=log(3/2), and let A consist of all source integers ≤M divisible by at least one prime of S, together with M to ensure the intended maximum. No source integer has three distinct primes of S. Inclusion-exclusion and Mertens give n/M→λ−λ²/2: singleton floor errors are at most |S|=o(M), pair floor errors at most |S|²=o(M), since π(sqrt M)^2=o(M); omitted diagonal reciprocal weight is o(1).

For each p∈S choose the target divisibility phase independently uniformly modulo p, simultaneously realized by a positive shift through the Chinese remainder theorem. For each target position the probability of being divisible by some S-prime is 1−prod_(p∈S)(1−1/p)→1−exp(−λ)=1/3. Some shift therefore has target union size at least (1/3−o(1))M. Use LP dual prices 1 on S and zero elsewhere, but use full valuations. The source weighted load is the total weighted load on [1,M], since every contributing source integer belongs to A. Its first-power part differs from the target first-power part by at most |S|=o(M); target valuations of higher powers are not necessarily bounded because the shift can be huge. To avoid this, choose any phase modulo p first and then choose a lift modulo p^4 avoiding every p^4 multiple in I. There are p³ lifts, but at most floor(M/p)+1 positions with the given phase, each forbidding at most one lift, and M/p+1<p³ for all these p and sufficiently large M. Each valuation is then ≤3, and the contributions of squares and cubes are O(M∑_(p∈S)1/p²+|S|)=o(M) for all such lifts. The union is unchanged. The dual value equals target union size plus source linear total minus target linear total, and hence is at least (1/3−o(1))M. Thus τ/n≥(1/3)/(λ−λ²/2)−o(1), strictly greater than 1 and below 2. This also supplies an order-M obstruction to uniform transfer of the capped dual load. It does not disprove the 2n or (2+o(1))n conjectures.

More generally use primes in (M^(1/3), M^(exp(λ)/3)] for fixed 0<λ≤log(3/2). The same argument gives a strict constant gap at every limiting source density λ−λ²/2 in (0,0.32326413…]. Indeed 1−e^(−λ)>λ−λ²/2 by Taylor's theorem. The endpoint ratio is 1.031148529….

Equivalent direct proof: any covering subset may omit at most the total excess valuation capacity across the band primes in positions divisible by at least one of them, because each omitted position spends at least one unit of that excess. No LP formalism is necessary.
