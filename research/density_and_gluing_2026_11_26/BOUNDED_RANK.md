# Two colors for arbitrary bounded rough rank outside a polynomial core

For each fixed positive integer R there is c_R>0 with the following property. For every 0<c≤c_R, suppose each source a can be written a=h u, X=M/h, with the primes of h all ≤z=X^c and all primes of u strictly greater than z, and ω(u)≤R. Then H(A,I)≤2n+o_R,c(n), uniformly in the shift and source density. All powers of the large primes are unrestricted. The polynomial-core result of [the two-atom sieve proof](../uniform_three_2026_11_26/POWER_CORE.md) treated R≤2; [the stretched-logarithmic result](../uniform_three_2026_11_26/BOUNDED_ROUGH_RANK.md) treated general fixed R with a stretched-logarithmic cutoff. The present argument uses those papers' full-atom balancing and elementary sieve estimates. The new ingredients are separation of the two types of representatives into colors and summing the second-block demand over all possible smooth cores before assigning those blocks.

As usual one can first apply the existing 2n result if M≥(2n)^3, so assume n≫M^(1/3). We may ignore the rows with X<(log M)^6 at a cost o(n) atoms. Indeed take c<1/12, so h is supported on primes ≤(log M)^(6c); there are at most (1+log_2 M)^π((log M)^(6c))=M^o(1) possible h, with at most (log M)^6 choices of u at each. Each such row has at most log_2 M atoms. The remaining X tend to infinity uniformly.

Balance the full atoms of u in two blocks by repeatedly placing the next largest full atom in the currently smaller product. Call the smaller final block v, the other w. Thus v≤sqrt X. In each nonempty block b, for every one of its full atoms q, b²≤X q: apply the balancing invariant at the last (smallest) atom added to b. In particular a block with at least two atoms has b≤X^(2/3). One red representative, unique across all rows, will carry h and v. A composite block w (here meaning at least two bases) will get one blue representative; a singleton w is reserved for final nested repair.

Put L=1+log(1/c). The reciprocal sum of products with at most R bases among powers of primes p∈(X^c,X] is ≤C_R L^R (include 1). Indeed Σ_{p>X^c,p≤X}1/(p−1)≤C+log(1/c) for large X, and expand by number of distinct bases. For T≥X^(1/3) the number of t≤T with all its primes >X^c and ω(t)≤R is at most C_R L^R T/log X. The integers t≤sqrt T cost O(sqrt T); for larger t choose a full atom P≥T^(1/(2R)) and fix the complementary integer k. The prime-power counting upper bound for P≤T/k is O_R((T/k)/log X) whenever T/k≥T^(1/(2R)); sum 1/k by the preceding reciprocal estimate. This implies m_{h,v}≤C_R L^R X/(v log X), and m_{h,w}≤C_R L^R X/(w log X) on composite w.

**Red Hall.** Red candidate sites are b=hv b' in I, with b' free of primes ≤z and coprime to v. The sets for different h are disjoint by the decreasing-cutoff prefix rule in [the two-atom sieve proof](../uniform_three_2026_11_26/POWER_CORE.md). Their uncolored sizes are ≥ k_0 X/(v log z) for c sufficiently small with absolute k_0: X/v≥sqrt X, the lower one-dimensional sieve applies and at most R((X/v)/z+1) sites fail the extra coprimality. Color every position independently fair red/blue; with positive probability, simultaneously every such red candidate set retains at least 1/3 of its sites in red, and every uncolored blue candidate set introduced below retains at least 1/3 in blue. Indeed there are O(M²) sets, each red set has size ≫sqrt X/log X≫log M for X≥(log M)^6; each blue bank has size ≫M^(1/3). Chernoff and a union bound suffice. Fix one such coloring.

For a collection of distinct v-labels at one h write λ=Σ 1/v, and D for the number of candidate labels at each site in the quotient interval of length X. The red first moment is ≥k_1 Xλ/(c log X). For all colors together,

    Σ D² ≤ C_R L^(2R) Xλ/(c log X).                                  (*)

Indeed, exact valuations on the primes of v imply that in a nonempty intersection each g=gcd(v,r) is a product of full atoms of v and of r. Write T=X/lcm(v,r)≥1 since both v,r≤sqrt X. By the upper one-dimensional sieve from [the two-atom sieve proof](../uniform_three_2026_11_26/POWER_CORE.md) the intersection has size O(T[1/log z+1/log(2T)]). For fixed v, Σ_r g/r≤2^R C_R L^R: choose the full-atom divisor g of v and sum 1/(r/g), including the diagonal. Thus the 1/log z term has the required bound. If g>1 then T≥g>z, so the second term is also already bounded. For coprime pairs order v≤r, doubling as needed. When v≤X^(1/4), T≥X^(1/4) and we are done. For v>X^(1/4), the reciprocal sum of all permitted r∈[v,sqrt X] is at most

    C_R L^R [1+log(sqrt X/v)]/log X.

Choose a full atom P of r of size ≥X^(1/(4R)), fix k=r/P. In each dyadic interval for P above X^(1/(4R)) its reciprocal prime-power mass is O_R(1/log X) by the prime-power counting bound; at most O(1+log(sqrt X/v)) such intervals are needed. Sum 1/k≤C_R L^R. Since log(2T)≥log(2sqrt X/v), the boundary contribution ordered at fixed v is O_R((X/v)L^R/log X). This proves (*), with the larger displayed power harmless.

Cauchy–Schwarz gives a red union size ≥ k_R Xλ/[c L^(2R) log X]. The total request count is ≤C_R L^R Xλ/log X. Take c L^(3R) sufficiently small in terms of R. Hall now assigns distinct red representatives at every h, and the disjoint cores combine.

**Blue multiplicity across all cores.** Every task for composite w chooses independently a uniform blue multiple b of w in the original interval I with gcd(b/w,w)=1. In contrast with red, h is not imposed at all. Uncolored such banks have ≥M/w−1−R(M/(w z_min)+1)≥M/(2w) sites for large M, where z_min=(log M)^(6c)→∞ and w≤M^(2/3). Blue banks therefore contain ≥M/(6w) sites. Two tasks sharing a base with incompatible powers have disjoint banks. Otherwise their gcd g>1 is a full-atom divisor of both. Their lcm≤M: w²≤M q, (w')²≤M q for a common full atom q, so ww'/g≤M. The probability of selecting the same site is O(g/M) since their intersection size is at most 2M/lcm(w,w'). No conflict between coprime labels is required.

For one task w its total probability of conflict with all neighbors is at most

    (C_R L^(2R)) Σ_{h : X=M/h≥(log M)^6, P⁺(h)≤X^c} 1/[h log X].

To see this, for each h bound m_{h,w'} by C_R L^R M/[h w' log X] above, divide by M, and sum g/w'. Choose g among at most 2^R full-atom divisors of the fixed w, and sum 1/(w'/g)≤C_R L^R on the independent rough scales X^c of that h. This upper bound permits identical tasks and all h, which can only increase the sum.

The displayed h-sum is at most Cc with an absolute C. Group X by 2^(−j−1)log M<log X≤2^(−j)log M. Set B_j=2^(−j)log M, y_j=exp(c B_j). All h in the group are y_j-smooth and h≥exp(log M−B_j); log X≥B_j/2. For y≥3, Σ_{P⁺(h)≤y} h^(−1+1/log y)≪log y uniformly for large y. Indeed divide the Euler product by ∏_{p≤y}(1−1/p)^(−1); the log ratio is O(Σ_{p≤y}(p^(1/log y)−1)/p+1)=O(1), since p^(1/log y)−1≤(e−1)log p/log y and Σ_{p≤y}log p/p=O(log y); Mertens bounds the undisturbed product. Thus the band contributes O(c exp[−(2^j−1)/c]). For occupied bands y_j≥(log M)^(6c)→∞ uniformly. Summing proves the assertion. The case h=1 is included in j=0.

Choose c so small that the per-task conflict probability is ≤1/8 for large M. The asymmetric local lemma with x_E=2 Pr(E) forbids every conflicting coincidence: neighboring events share a task, their total x is ≤1/2, so ∏(1−x)≥1/2. Different second tasks may then share a blue site only across disjoint prime bases. All paid atoms have per-prime distinct credits because blue and red are disjoint and red is globally injective. Restore omitted singleton w and exceptional source atoms by nested level repair; at each base the greatest remaining target valuations cover all levels for a cost at most its number of missing atoms since full-interval divisibility counts dominate the source at every level. Every nonexceptional row has one red site and at most one further blue site or repair. This proves the theorem.

A simpler useful variant replaces the adaptive scale by fixed z=M^c, assumes h is the full z-smooth part and h≤M^(1−κ) on every row for fixed κ>0. The same proof works for c sufficiently small in terms of R,κ. Here X≥M^κ, z is between X^c and X^(c/κ), the red sieve and Cauchy margin cost at most O_R((c/κ)L^(3R)), and Σ_h 1/(h log X)≤O((log z)/(κ log M))=O(c/κ).
