# Fixed-gap inheritance by the complementary source after bounded-rank removal

Let C be either the ordinary product covering number τ or the level covering number H. Let J=[1,M], let I be any positive integer interval with M positions, and let n=|A|≤M/2. Fix δ>0 (one may decrease δ to ≤1), a positive integer R, and density lower bound t>0. If n≥tM and C(A,I)>(2+δ)n, then for all large M and sufficiently small fixed c=c(R,δ,t)>0 the following holds. Remove ALL rows G satisfying the adaptive polynomial-core hypothesis in [the two-color construction](BOUNDED_RANK.md) at rank R and exponent c, and let E=A minus G. Then

    C(E,I)>2|E|+(δ/2)n.

This is an inheritance theorem for the actual covering number on the complement, including the ordinary product. It does not assume a density estimate for G. A counterexample with n>M/2 is impossible because the entire I is a cover of size M. One may shorten I to a subwindow of length max E if one wants the normal max=window-length convention.

**The witness.** Introduce a nonnegative unit-cost slack for each prime in the product covering LP. In the level LP use just one unit-cost slack s_p per prime jointly in all of its level inequalities. Rounding the fractional positions at a basic optimum upwards costs at most the number of resource inequalities, o(n) at positive density. Given all these selected positions, the minimal extra repair at each prime is bounded by the ceiling of its LP slack (take available positions of largest valuation in the level case, in which the maximal unserved level deficit suffices). Even for fractional slacks this gives total rounding cost O(number of inequalities). Indeed, floor-integrality of each remaining integer deficit in fact gives at most floor(s_p). Thus duality supplies nonnegative prices w having Φ_A=Σ_A X−T>(2+3δ/4)n, T=Σ_I(X−1)_+. For ordinary product X=Σ w_p v_p with w_p≤1; for levels X=Σ w_pj 1[p^j divides u] with Σ_j w_pj≤1. In the ordinary model the LP slack constraint gives precisely the price bound 0≤w_p≤1.

For ordinary product keep *uncapped* target valuations in its LP. Every source coordinate still has one constraint per prime, regardless of a target valuation. All witness lower bounds then hold for the true cover by summing its uncapped attained valuations. For levels only levels up to floor(log_p M) occur. The exponent moment and reciprocal estimates below use upper bounds on source loads only and do not require capped target valuations.

The witness has T≤C n log(eM/n), uniformly, by the following elementary bound. Since target valuations at all source levels dominate those on J by counts, Σ_{J outside A}X≤M−Φ_A≤M. Put W(u)=Σ_{p≤M^(1/3)} w_{p,1} 1[p|u], with w_{p,1}=w_p in the product case. Let μ=Σ_{p≤M^(1/3)} w_{p,1}/p. On uniform J the mean of W is μ+O(M^(−2/3)), and its variance is ≤μ+o(1) from floor counts on pairs and diagonals (or the upper second moment μ²+μ). The condition n≤M/2 implies 1≥E[W 1_{J outside A}]≥μ/2−O(1)−sqrt(μ+1), so μ=O(1). The remaining first-level Mertens mass is bounded absolutely. Consequently there is absolute s>0 with E_J exp(sX)=O(1). To verify, dominate both loads by Σ_p [w_{p,1}1[p|u]+(v_p(u)−1)_+], expand the exponential in nonnegative divisibility increments at each prime and replace floor(M/d)/M by 1/d. Each Euler factor is 1+O(w_{p,1}/p+1/p²) for s=log(4/3). Jensen now gives Σ_A X≤C n log(eM/n), hence the same bound for T. At density t fixed this is C_t n.

**Reserve only a fraction of the hinge.** Color target positions independently red, blue, or unused with probabilities α/2,α/2,1−α where α≤δ/(16C_t). All red and blue lists in the bounded-rank power-core argument keep at least α/4 of their original size in the respective color simultaneously with probability 1−o(1), by Chernoff and the polynomially many lists. The minimum uncolored red size is ≫(log M)^3/log log M and blue size ≫M^(1/3). Moreover the sum of (X−1)_+ on the union U of the red and blue sites has mean αT, so with probability ≥1/2 it is at most 2αT (if T=0 this is automatic). Fix a coloring satisfying both.

The Hall first moment keeps its α/4 fraction, and the all-site second moment is unchanged; expansion is ≫_R α²/[c(1+log(1/c))^(3R)]. The independent blue hash conflicts have incident sum ≪_R α^(−2)c(1+log(1/c))^(2R). Choose c small enough to pass both tests. The colored construction produces at most two distinct paid positions per row, credits may share blue positions only at disjoint bases. For a singleton outside block w=p^j, charge its original row one extra unit of price instead. For levels its full contribution is ≤1 from the dual constraint. For product prices it is ≤1+(j−1)_+, and the total extra premium on all retained rows is ≤Σ_{p>z_min}Σ_{j≥2}floor(M/p^j)=O(M/z_min)=o(n), where z_min=(log M)^(6c) from dropping the negligible small-X cores. Exceptional rows have vanishing total ordinary price as they are M^o(1) in number. Every covered site credits total price at most X(b), by distinct bases across blue tasks at the same position and red injectivity. Summing gives Σ_G X≤2|G|+Σ_U(X−1)_++o(n)≤2|G|+2αT+o(n). Thus Φ_E=Φ_A−Σ_G X>2|E|+(δ/2)n, and every feasible cover of E has size at least Φ_E by dual weak bounds.

**Polylog-density version.** If n≥M/(log M)^K, K>0 fixed, the same conclusion holds after an initial peeling of primes >4n followed by deleting all good rows at exponent c=(log log M)^(−4). If initial positive density is assumed one can omit this first peeling entirely. Specifically remove successively any row divisible by p>4n. Here p>4n>sqrt M at this density, so p occurs once. In reverse order, choose a fresh multiple of h=a/p≤M/(4n) outside the current cover (I has ≥4n such multiples). If p still has a deficit, a remaining p-position exists since the whole interval meets the demand. This argument works for both actual notions of cover; if C(A_0,I)≤2|A_0|+δn, the covering budget during reverse restoration remains at most 3n. Hence C(A_0,I)>2|A_0|+δn. Every retained source prime is ≤4n. Number of ordinary/level LP inequalities is then O(n/log n+sqrt M log M)=o(n), validating the above witness now with A_0 and the same absolute n in its gap. Jensen gives T≤C n log(eM/n), using monotonicity in |A_0|. Choose α=δ/[16C log(eM/n)]. Then α^(−2)c(1+log(1/c))^(3R)=o(1) for fixed R.

Use cutoff X_min=exp((K+2)(log log M)^5) for the new c, so z_min=(log M)^(K+2), and uniformity in all previous sieve/Mertens bounds holds (the lower-sieve level ratio only improves). Rows with X<X_min have h supported on primes ≤z_min and at most X_min possibilities for u. By Rankin at σ=1−1/[2(K+2)], the number of z_min-smooth h≤M is ≤M^σ exp(O_K(sqrt(log M))) since the log Euler product is O_K(z_min^(1−σ)). So the excluded count times maximum price is o(n). The total singleton premium O(M/z_min) is o(n). Bank-size union bounds remain valid with α≳1/log log M. We conclude C(E,I)>2|E|+(δ/2)n where E is the remainder after both stages. For the fixed-global cutoff variant, the same proof applies with y=M^c and h≤M^(1−κ) for fixed κ>0; no small-X exception is needed and M/y=o(n). This theorem does not assert hardness for any particular quotient.

**All rows with bounded rough rank can be removed in the fixed-global variant.** Let y=M^c and let H_y(a) be the entire y-smooth part of a, with multiplicity. Under the uniform law on J,

    E H_y(a)^(1/log y) ≤ ∏_{p≤y} (1−1/p)/(1−p^(−1+1/log y)) ≤ C

for y large, with an absolute C. The first bound follows from the nonnegative prime-power divisor increments of the observable, the second from Mertens' estimate Σ_{p≤y}log p/p=O(log y) and p^(1/log y)−1≤(e−1)log p/log y. Consequently at most m≤CM exp(−1/(2c)) integers in J have H_y(a)>sqrt M. The total X-witness load on any m source rows is at most C' m log(eM/m) by the same uniform exponential moment as above. At fixed density t, reducing c in terms of R,δ,t makes this ≤δ n/16. At n≥M/(log M)^K and c=(log log M)^(−4) it is o(n), since exp(−(log log M)^4/2) decays faster than every fixed inverse power of log M. Select α with another factor of two in hand if necessary (e.g. δ/[64C log(eM/n)]); one retains a margin greater than δn/2 at the end.

Remove all bounded rough-rank rows with H_y≤sqrt M using the fixed-global construction (κ=1/2); then the further bounded rough-rank rows with H_y>sqrt M cost at most the displayed δn/16 in price. Therefore for both ordinary product and level coverage the residual E obeys C(E,I)>2|E|+(δ/2)n and EVERY a in E has at least R+1 distinct prime factors >M^c. At positive density E may be obtained simply by throwing away all rows with at most R such primes. In the polylog regime first remove all rows with a prime >4n; then delete all bounded rough-rank rows. No claim requires that the rare smooth-tail rows were originally few relative to some intermediate remainder.

**Wider near-linear density regimes.** Put L=log M. For ordinary τ, fix 0<a<1/3, 2a<b<1−a, n/M≥exp(−L^a), and y=exp(L^(1−b)). The same fixed-global conclusion (every retained row has at least R+1 primes >y and retains gap δn/2) follows with α a small δ-dependent constant times L^(−a), c=L^(−b). The previous red and blue conditions reduce to O_R(L^(2a−b)(log L)^(3R))=o(1); all global banks have polynomial size because h≤sqrt M. The exceptional smooth-part contribution divided by n goes to zero by b>a. The singleton repeat contribution O(M/y)=o(n) by 1−b>a. First peeling and LP rounding work since n=M^(1−o(1)), and the sparse color union bound still holds. For the stronger H conclusion from an H gap, the common slack per base charges a missing singleton only one regardless of its exponent, so the last inequality is unnecessary: 0<a<1/2 and any 2a<b<1 are sufficient. The H version from τ(A,I)>(2+δ)n guarantees a residual level-cover gap and does not by itself imply an ordinary-product gap there.
