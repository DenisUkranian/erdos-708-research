# Doubling the density of a hard pair fiber

Assume 0<δ≤1 and t=n/M≤1/2 with t≥(log M)^(−K), K fixed. For positive density allow t≥t0. Use the notation and calibrated level dual of [the preceding inverse theorem](../fiber_selection_2026_11_26/PAIR_INCREMENT.md). Set γ=δ/1024, z=M^(1−γ), L=Σ_{z<q≤M}1/q≤δ/256 for sufficiently large M. This follows from −log(1−γ)≤2γ and an o(1) error. The number of retained level constraints r=o(n). The comparison of f and f(pv) works at every d=p^j≤z since quotient lengths are at least floor(M^γ). For t≥log^(−K) M and either s≤2 exp((log M)^a) (fixed a<1) or s≤2 exp(log M / [(log log M)(log log log M)^2]), the profile error there is ≪_K (log M)^(−K−2). For t≥t0 and s≤2M^α, first pick α≪γ small enough that all weighted transfer errors are ≤δn/100. Contributions of powers d>z are o(n), because they have exponent ≥2 and Σ_{d>z,powers≥2} C_d≪ M log M / sqrt(z). At p>y the remaining higher powers cost O(M/y)=o(n). Large-prime source hits excluded by S0 cost ≤|S0|M/z=o(n) as long as α is sufficiently small. Take the population cutoff δ n log M/(256 z) and weak-marginal cutoff (1+δ/64)tL_p exactly as before. Their cost is ≤δn/64+10δn/64 eventually. By reducing α or enlarging M, the irreversible losses before this filtering are ≤δn/16, and π(z)/M rounding in μ contributes o(n) in the resulting total.

Use X for the actual small-level dual load and retain the comparison slack
R=Σ_I[(X−1)_+−f(X−1)]≥0.
For the final filtered P, Y=Σ_{p∈P} w_p 1_{p|b}, μ=Σ_P w_p/p<10. Then
R ≥ Σ_I (1−f)(X−1) ≥ Σ_I(1−f)(Y−1).
The last expression is at least (1−t)M(μ−1)−δn/32: indeed |Σ_I f−n| is small, Σ_I Y≥Mμ−π(z), and Σ_I fY=tMμ+O(εMμ+π(z)) by the same calibrated transfer, since all p∈P are outside S and ≤z. Take the profile errors and cutoff/rank errors sufficiently small. Therefore R≥(1−t)M(μ−1)_+−δn/32. Keeping this slack in the original proof yields
u:=E_A Z ≥ 1+μ+δ/2 + [(1−t)/t](μ−1)_+,
where Z=Y+1_{there is a terminal prime outside S0}. This uses τ failure only through the stronger H failure. If using the uncalibrated f=1 baseline on its own, ensure that the canonical contributions from discarded or conditioned primes do not reappear with the wrong sign. The slack proof avoids that issue.

Let u0=1+μ for μ≤1 and u0=2μ otherwise. Then u≥u0+δ/2, and u0(u0−1)≥2μ² in both cases. The normalized actual pair count is ≥u(u−1)/2, whereas the full feasible background is ≤μ²/2+μL. Set λ=2+δ/64. The weighted surplus over λ times *all* pair backgrounds is ≥
 n[ δ(2u0−1)/4 − δ μ²/128 − (2+δ/64) μ L ]
 ≥ (δ/8) n max(1,μ).
For μ≤1 use 2u0−1≥1, μL≤δ/256, and μ²≤1. For 1≤μ≤10 use 2u0−1=4μ−1≥3μ, μ²≤10μ and L≤δ/256. Nonpositive edges may be removed, increasing the surplus. This proves a marginal-qualified pair with canonical density >λt outside ANY allowed S0.

If t log y→∞, use H0=exp(δ t log y/1024) and discard pq>M/H0, where both endpoints exceed y. For each fixed smaller p the reciprocal sum of possible q in the boundary strip is ≤log H0/log y+O(1/log y)≤δ t/512 eventually. Thus the source loss is ≤μ δ n/512, leaving surplus ≥δn/10 even with the cutoff. For positive density we may take ρ=δ t0 α/1024. Let c_e=w_p v_q, A*=Σ c_e C_e, J*=Σ c_e L_e for retained hot pairs. Then A*≥δn/10 and A*≥λtJ*. We can choose a union with source density >λ't where λ'=2+δ/128: discard high-load tails ≤δ A*/4096 using the exponential moment bound in [the preceding inverse theorem](../fiber_selection_2026_11_26/PAIR_INCREMENT.md). On the remaining rows with Y≤K*=C_δ D/log(2+D) choose edges independently with probabilities θc_e, θ≤δ/[2048(K*+1)^2]. The target gain on the source is ≥θ(1−δ/1024)A*, the expected canonical union size ≤θJ*. Since 1−λ'/λ=δ/(128λ)>δ/260, some union gains ≥δ θ A*/512 over λ't times its size (all constants absorb the tail). This gives |A∩V|≫_δ n[log(2+D)/D]^2. Here D=1+log(M/n). For one center, orient by the smaller prime and extract p with outgoing source weight ≥δ t L_p/100 since Σ w_p L_p≤10M. At this quotient the outgoing reciprocal mass ≤10+L, so truncate the same tail bound and thin with θ≤δ/[2048(K*+1)], proving one star of density >λ't and source population ≫_δ t L_p log(2+D)/D.

At t≥1/4 every retained pair fiber has density >1/2 and is consequently covered within 2C_e using the entire quotient interval (integer +1 endpoint harmless at polynomial fiber sizes). The common-p star is itself more than half full in its canonical union but the union of many prime progressions still needs target transfer. For polynomial n=M^(β+o(1)), keep γ with 1−γ<β and adjust for terminal reciprocal mass; in general γ cannot be chosen on the scale of an arbitrarily tiny δ for fixed β<1. The argument as stated claims the doubled factor only for logarithmically sparse or positive-density sources.
