# Trimming candidate lists before sparse color reservation

This sharpens the thinning estimate in [the residual price proof](RESIDUAL_GAP.md) for a fixed global threshold y=M^c, full y-smooth part h≤sqrt M, X=M/h∈[sqrt M,M], with c→0, y→∞. Write L=log M, L_c=1+log(1/c), R fixed. Let C_{h,v} be the uncolored red bank, and C_w the uncolored global blue bank of [the two-color proof](BOUNDED_RANK.md). Distinct h have disjoint red banks. The original candidate multisets have demands m_{h,v} or m_w at each distinct label. Upper full-rank multiplicity bounds m_{h,v}≤C_R L_c^R X/(v log X) and |C_{h,v}|≥c_0 X/(v log y) imply

    m_{h,v}/|C_{h,v}| ≪_R c L_c^R.

For blue also create a task for any singleton w=p^j with j≥2 and w≤M^(1/6). Such a w≤X^(1/3), so the multiplicity estimate per h and all conditions in the blue conflict calculation remain valid. Every other deferred repeated singleton has w>M^(1/6); the sum of unit valuations on all such source rows is O(M^(11/12)(log M)^3)=o(n) when n=M^(1−o(1)), from Σ_{p^j>M^(1/6),j≥2}1/p^j≪M^(−1/12)log M (dyadic prime-power counting). Nonrepeated singletons cost at most one in either dual.

For red define the full-site congestion Y_h(t)=Σ_r (m_{h,r}/|C_{h,r}|)1[t∈C_{h,r}]. For each fixed v,

    (1/|C_{h,v}|) Σ_{t∈C_{h,v}} Y_h(t) ≪_R c L_c^(2R) [1+c log log(3M)] =: ρ_red.

Indeed it suffices to bound Σ_r |C_{h,v}∩C_{h,r}|. As in the base proof, compatible g=gcd(v,r) is unitary in both, T=X/lcm(v,r)≥1 and each intersection is ≪T[1/log y+1/log(2T)]. The first summand or the second when g>1 costs O_R((X/v)L_c^R/log y). If gcd(v,r)=1 and min(v,r)≤X^(1/4), T≥X^(1/4), handled identically. Otherwise fix v; the full range of remaining r is within (X^(1/4),sqrt X]. Subdivide it by 2^j≤T<2^(j+1). In each such multiplicative band the r-reciprocal mass is O_R(L_c^R/log X), by choosing a full prime-power atom ≥X^(1/(4R)), fixing the complement, and summing reciprocal prime powers on the resulting dyadic interval; the complement reciprocal sum is O_R(L_c^R). Summing 1/(j+1) costs O(log log X). This yields O_R((X/v)L_c^R log log(3X)/log X) for the boundary. Divide by the red size and use the above load-per-label bound.

Remove from each red bank all sites with Y_h(t)>2ρ_red (take explicit sufficiently large absolute R-dependent constants in ρ_red). At least half of each bank survives. For blue tasks define the competitor load Y_w(t)=Σ_{w':gcd(w,w')>1} (m_w'/|C_w'|)1[t∈C_w']; this sum may include the task's own label. Its mean on C_w is ≪_R c L_c^(2R)=:ρ_blue by exactly the global unitary-divisor conflict calculation of the base proof, with no sparse coloring yet. Remove from C_w all t with Y_w(t)>2ρ_blue, retaining at least half.

Color independently red and blue with probabilities α/2 each, leaving the other sites unused. Every one of the pruned banks retains Ω(α) of its ORIGINAL size with probability 1−o(1) if α^(-1) is polylogarithmic: the original sizes are polynomial in M here and there are only polynomially many banks. Simultaneously the total dual hinge of used colors is ≤2αT by Markov with probability at least 1/2. Fix a successful coloring. Uniform fractional placement of every red task on its final bank has total load per site ≤Cρ_red/α: any site used by even one surviving label satisfies the global inequality Y_h(t)≤2ρ_red. If this is <1 the fractional placement proves Hall's condition for every task subset; an integral matching follows. For blue independent choices, at any possible selected site for w, the aggregate conditional probability of a conflict with some other task is ≤Cρ_blue/α by the site's own deletion test. With this <1/8 per task, the asymmetric local lemma used in the base proof gives a simultaneous conflict-free assignment. Markov's chosen coloring still supplies the objective bound. Hence the condition on both colors is now only

    c L_c^(2R) [1+c log log(3M)] = o(α).

**Both covering numbers.** For any fixed 0<a<1, a<b<1 and n/M≥exp(−L^a), take c=L^(−b), y=exp(L^(1−b)), α a small δ-dependent constant times L^(−a). Then ρ_red+ρ_blue=o(α) and the smooth-tail mass m log(eM/m) with m≪M exp(−1/(2c)) is o(n). The repeated-singleton bound above is o(n), so for H and ordinary τ deleting all low rank rows, after p>4n peel, leaves E with C(E,I)>2|E|+(δ/2)n and at least R+1 different primes >y in every row. At critical n≥M/(log M)^K one can alternatively choose c=1/[D_0 (log D_0)^(2R+1)] with D_0=log log M, α≍_{δ,K}1/D_0, improving the baseline inverse-fourth power.

**Arbitrary near-linear source sizes for ordinary product.** Write D=1+log(M/n), where D=o(L). The actual ordinary dual may be chosen to satisfy T≤C n min(D,L/D) whenever D>10 log L; for all D one has T≤C n sqrt L. Indeed if D>10 log L, for each p with log p<D/4 choose k=floor(D/(2log p))≥D/(4log p). The subset of at least floor(M/p^k) targets divisible by p^k supplies total p-valuation ≥k floor(M/p^k)≥nL/log p≥E_p(A_0), by e^(D/2)≫L/D and large M. Whenever the ordinary dual coordinate w_p exceeds 1/k, lower it down to 1/k. All these targets remain at total load at least one during the change, so their hinge drops by at least E_p times the price decrease; the objective does not decrease. This imposes every cap jointly, since each argument only uses w_p itself. For remaining p, w_p≤1≤4log p/D. This gives X(a)≤4log a/D≤4L/D for every source. Combine with the entropy bound O(nD); when D≤10 log L that bound alone gives O(n sqrt L). This cap argument is the elementary high-level price cap from [Wang, dual pivot analysis](https://github.com/science-prince/erdos-708-interval-divisor-covers/blob/fc929346e7dbe0e818070ef90e4dfbfa06169527/evidence/research/round04/S05_dual_pivot_wave2.md), specialized to the raw original ordinary LP.

Take V=max(D,sqrt L), c=(L V)^(−1/2), y=exp(sqrt(L/V)), α=δ/(C' sqrt L) with large absolute C'. Then y→∞ even when D/L tends to zero arbitrarily slowly. Also c≤L^(−3/4), c L_c^(2R) sqrt L→0, and 1/c=√(LV)≫D, so the canonical smooth-tail contribution is o(n). This proves the same actual ordinary τ-gap inheritance with every residual row having at least R+1 distinct prime divisors >y, for EVERY sequence n=M^(1−o(1)). When one knows n/M≥exp(−L^a), the previous paragraph gives a much larger y.

Prior comparators: the pure-rank/polynomial construction is in [the two-color proof](BOUNDED_RANK.md); Hall and local-lemma pruning isolates the repeated sites where the naive two-color cost would have been quadratic in 1/α. Zero-containing intervals are trivial and negative intervals reflect, so all statements are read for positive I.
