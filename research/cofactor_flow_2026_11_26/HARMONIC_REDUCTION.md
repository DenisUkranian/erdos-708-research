# A harmonic cut criterion for one-deletion matching

Write H_M for the M-th harmonic number. In a positive interval I of M integers let B_d be the positions divisible by d. Put

    e(M,I) = max over T ⊆ I of [ (1/H_M) sum_{d≤M : B_d⊆T} 1/d − |T|/M ].

The maximum is nonnegative. For any source family A ⊆ [2,M], join a to every target divisible by one of its full-prime-power cofactors a/p^{v_p(a)}. Every such graph has a matching leaving at most M e(M,I) + O(M/log M) rows unmatched, uniformly over shifts and families.

**Deletion law.** Take U uniform in [1,M]. For U≥2 delete one occurrence of a prime p chosen with probability v_p(U) log p / log U, and put D=U/p; set D=1 for U=1. The total variation distance from the law P(Y=d)=1/(dH_M) is O(1/log M). Indeed, replace log U in the denominator by log M. This changes the law in l1 by at most M^(-1) + (M log M)^(-1) sum_{u=2}^M log(M/u) = O(1/log M). The new mass at d is (M log M)^(-1) times the sum of (1+v_p(d)) log p over primes p≤M/d. The extra valuation terms cost O(1/log M) in l1 by summing over p^j | U, j≥2, and using convergence of sum_p log p / (p(p−1)). The main mass is θ(M/d)/(M log M). Its l1 difference from 1/(d log M) is O(1/log M). To see this, group M/d into the dyadic bands [2^r,2^{r+1}); the sum over d of 1/d in each nonempty band is bounded by an absolute constant, and the de la Vallée Poussin prime number theorem bounds the relative error on that band by O(exp(−c sqrt(r))) for r≥1. Include the band r=0 separately. Finally H_M=log M+O(1).

**Cut.** If S is a subset of A and U belongs to S, then B_D ⊆ N(S). In fact D is a multiple of the cofactor obtained by deleting the entire power of the sampled prime. Hence |S|/M ≤ P(B_D ⊆ N(S)) ≤ |N(S)|/M + e(M,I) + O(1/log M). Apply the deficiency form of Hall's theorem. Given an injection on the matched rows, one selected point per row covers all but possibly one atom there; adding one point per unpaid prime atom suffices by nested prime-power counts. Thus the matched rows have a cover of size at most twice their number. For |A|=Θ(M) and |A|≤M/2, the weighted source-removal stability inequality in the redlydi residue-certificates report, followed by LP rounding with at most π(M) positions, allows o(M) unmatched rows. Consequently e(M,I)=o(1) uniformly in I would settle all fixed positive densities. It has not been proved here. The entire interval works when |A|≥M/2.

**Two boundaries.** Fix S and let E be the set of all multiples of its cofactor generators. The independent geometric prime model G has P(G in E) equal to the periodic natural density of E. Conditional on G≤M its distribution is exactly harmonic, since P(G=d) is a constant times 1/d for d≤M. Harris's inequality applied to the increasing event E and the decreasing event G≤M shows that harmonic mass of E is at most that natural density. Thus the mean over a full shift period satisfies |S|≤ mean |N(S)| + O(M/log M), uniformly in S. This comparison does not bound a cut chosen after observing a particular shift. Also, for an odd-length interval centered at a common multiple of 1,...,M, a singleton T traps every d>M/2. Hence e(M,I)≥(log 2+o(1))/log M, a scale that may already exceed n/M along sparse sequences.

This argument uses prime deletion as in the coupling literature (cf. R. Arratia, arXiv:1305.0941). The fixed-set Davenport–Erdős theorem on sets of multiples does not provide the uniform estimate above for families that change with M.
