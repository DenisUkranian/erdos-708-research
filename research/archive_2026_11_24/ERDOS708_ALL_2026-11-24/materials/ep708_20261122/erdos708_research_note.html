Erdős Problem 708:
Verified Partial Arguments and the Remaining Gap

Working research note prepared for Denis Paliy

22 November 2026

Abstract

We give a self-contained whole-cofactor extension argument valid at every interval length, including a saving for each distinct largest prime among the nonsmooth sources. Combined with a boxed linear relaxation, it gives a conditional bound g(n)≤2n+π(qn)−1g(n)\le 2n+\pi(q_n)-1, where qn=min{Z≥2:Z≥2n+π(Z)−1}q_n=\min\{Z\ge2:Z\ge2n+\pi(Z)-1\}, from the global hinge inequality (H2)(H_2). Thus this conditional route has no remaining interval-size gap. An unconditional consequence covers sets whose every member has a prime factor greater than 2n−12n-1. We also give exact obstructions to two overly strong proof strategies, including a family on which a particular independent-prime charging bound has unbounded relative error while a valid cover uses 2n−22n-2 positions. Proofs, computations, assumptions, and attribution are separated throughout.

The intended problem and the proof boundary

Let A⊂ℤ≥2A\subset\mathbb Z_{\ge2} have nn distinct elements. Put M=maxAM=\max A and Ix,M={x+1,…,x+M},x∈ℤ≥0.I_{x,M}=\{x+1,\ldots,x+M\},\qquad x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}. Define τ(A,Ix,M)=min{|B|:B⊆Ix,M,∏a∈Aa∣∏b∈Bb},g(n)=supA,xτ(A,Ix,M).\tau(A,I_{x,M})=\min\left\{|B|:B\subseteq I_{x,M},\quad \prod_{a\in A}a\mid\prod_{b\in B}b\right\}, \qquad g(n)=\sup_{A,x}\tau(A,I_{x,M}). Every chosen interval position is used at most once. We use the intended “at most” convention, not a literal exact-cardinality wording that can fail merely because an interval is too short. The questions are g(n)≤?2n,g(n)≤?(2+o(1))n.g(n)\stackrel{?}{\le}2n,\qquad g(n)\stackrel{?}{\le}(2+o(1))n. The recent finite-case paper of Tripodo explicitly leaves the general question open . This note addresses that general question, without presenting small cases or a conditional reduction as its solution.

Several arguments below work more generally with M≥maxAM\ge\max A. The whole interval is always a cover, because ∏a∈Aa∣M!,∏b∈Ix,Mb=M!(x+MM).\prod_{a\in A}a\mid M!,\qquad \prod_{b\in I_{x,M}}b=M!\binom{x+M}{M}. Consequently M≤2nM\le2n is immediate. Intervals containing zero are trivial; all-negative intervals reduce to positive intervals by reflection.

Whole-cofactor extension without a scale restriction

The next elementary lemma will keep repeated prime powers properly paid.

The exact missing inequality and a conditional consequence

For weights wp∈[0,1]w_p\in[0,1] on primes p≤Mp\le M, set Xw(k)=∑p≤Mwpvp(k),u+=max(u,0).X_w(k)=\sum_{p\le M}w_pv_p(k),\qquad u_+=\max(u,0). The unproved global inequality in this route is ∑a=1M(Xw(a)−2)+≤∑b∈Ix,M(Xw(b)−1)+for every M,x,w.\tag{$H_2$}\label{eq:h2} \sum_{a=1}^{M}\left(X_w(a)-2\right)_+ \le \sum_{b\in I_{x,M}}\left(X_w(b)-1\right)_+ \quad\text{for every }M,x,w. We do not prove [eq:h2] here. The following consequences keep it as an explicit hypothesis.

Boxed relaxation and dual clipping

For Dp(A)=∑a∈Avp(a)D_p(A)=\sum_{a\in A}v_p(a), define L(A,I)=min{∑b∈Izb:0≤zb≤1,∑b∈Ivp(b)zb≥Dp(A) for all demanded p}.L(A,I)=\min\left\{\sum_{b\in I}z_b: 0\le z_b\le1,\quad\sum_{b\in I}v_p(b)z_b\ge D_p(A) \text{ for all demanded }p\right\}. The upper boxes zb≤1z_b\le1 are essential: an interval position cannot be used repeatedly. The term “raw” in the cited source refers to the coefficients vp(b)v_p(b), not to removal of those boxes. The feasible set is nonempty by the whole-interval observation. Finite linear-programming duality gives L(A,I)=maxwp≥0[∑a∈AXw(a)−∑b∈I(Xw(b)−1)+].\label{eq:dual} L(A,I)=\max_{w_p\ge0}\left[ \sum_{a\in A}X_w(a)-\sum_{b\in I}\left(X_w(b)-1\right)_+\right]. If wp=1+δ>1w_p=1+\delta>1, lower it to one. For every multiple of pp the right hinge remains in its linear region. Its total decrease is exactly δ∑b∈Ivp(b)\delta\sum_{b\in I}v_p(b), whereas the source term decreases by δDp(A)\delta D_p(A). The former is at least the latter, because ∑b∈Ivp(b)≥∑e≥1⌊M/pe⌋≥Dp(A).\sum_{b\in I}v_p(b) \ge\sum_{e\ge1}\lfloor M/p^e\rfloor \ge D_p(A). Thus clipping cannot reduce the dual objective, and weights in [0,1][0,1] suffice. If [eq:h2] holds, then ∑a∈AXw(a)≤2|A|+∑a=1M(Xw(a)−2)+≤2|A|+∑b∈I(Xw(b)−1)+.\sum_{a\in A}X_w(a) \le 2|A|+\sum_{a=1}^M\left(X_w(a)-2\right)_+ \le 2|A|+\sum_{b\in I}\left(X_w(b)-1\right)_+. Therefore L(A,I)≤2|A|L(A,I)\le2|A|.

Conversely, if L(A,I)≤2|A|L(A,I)\le2|A| holds for all source subsets of {1,…,M}\{1,\ldots,M\}, apply [eq:dual] to A={a≤M:Xw(a)>2}A=\{a\le M:X_w(a)>2\} to recover [eq:h2]. The hinge formulation is therefore a substantive covering statement, not an innocuous estimate that can be omitted from the proof.

Rounding and all interval lengths

Theorem 4 (Conditional; assumes global (H2)(H_2)). Let qn=min{Z∈ℤ:Z≥2,Z≥2n+π(Z)−1}.q_n=\min\{Z\in\mathbb Z:Z\ge2,\quad Z\ge2n+\pi(Z)-1\}. If the global inequality [eq:h2] holds, then g(n)≤2n+π(qn)−1=2n+(2+o(1))nlogn.g(n)\le 2n+\pi(q_n)-1 =2n+(2+o(1))\frac{n}{\log n}. In particular the asymptotic question would be answered for every interval length. This does not prove the exact inequality g(n)≤2ng(n)\le2n.

Proof. The minimum exists: Z=4nZ=4n works since π(4n)≤2n\pi(4n)\le2n. Split the sources at Z=qnZ=q_n, and let mm be the number of smooth sources. If m>0m>0, solve their boxed relaxation. An optimal vertex has at most π(Z)\pi(Z) fractional coordinates: otherwise its fractional columns are dependent on the tight demand rows, permitting a nonzero two-sided feasible perturbation. Rounding all fractional coordinates up preserves feasibility. If some coordinate is fractional, the increase is strictly less than their number; if none is fractional, the solution is already integral. Since L≤2mL\le2m and π(Z)≥1\pi(Z)\ge1, both cases give |B0|≤2m+π(Z)−1.|B_0|\le2m+\pi(Z)-1. If m=0m=0, take B0=⌀B_0=\varnothing instead. For m=0m=0 it is even at most 2n−12n-1. The extension theorem applies at every MM and gives the finite bound. A collision charging bound cannot close the problem by itself

Choose, for each a∈Aa\in A, an interval multiple bab_a. Let tt be the number of distinct chosen multiples and let SbS_b be the union of the prime supports of sources assigned to bb. The known collision bound is τ(A,I)≤U:=t+∑a∈Aω(a)−∑b|Sb|,\label{eq:hub} \tau(A,I)\le U:=t+\sum_{a\in A}\omega(a)-\sum_b|S_b|, where ω\omega counts distinct prime factors . For completeness, for each prime pp the source-to-target graph matching aa to multiples of pvp(a)p^{v_p(a)} satisfies Hall by nested neighborhoods: #{a:pe∣a}≤⌊M/pe⌋≤#{b∈I:pe∣b}.\#\{a:p^e\mid a\}\le\lfloor M/p^e\rfloor \le\#\{b\in I:p^e\mid b\}. Start a partial matching by assigning the largest pp-valuation source in each relevant collision group to its chosen multiple. Augmenting paths extend it to a full matching while preserving the set of matched right vertices, although individual assignments can change. If kpk_p sources contain pp and cpc_p such groups exist, this adds kp−cpk_p-c_p new right vertices. Taking their union over pp proves [eq:hub].

Unbounded overestimate on a whole family

Two analytical shortcuts that do not discharge (H2)(H_2)

A false sparse numerical comparison and a valid replacement

The sparse binary argument in uses the comparison μ1+μ>μ2+σ22,μ=∑p∈S1p<1,σ2=∑p∈S1p2.\frac{\mu}{1+\mu}>\frac{\mu^2+\sigma_2}{2}, \qquad \mu=\sum_{p\in S}\frac1p<1, \quad \sigma_2=\sum_{p\in S}\frac1{p^2}. Reproduction and final status

The accompanying archive contains this LaTeX source, its PDF, Python verification scripts, and machine-readable output. No third-party paper sources are bundled. The standard-library checks can be run with

python checks/verify_exact_certificates.py
python checks/verify_hub_obstruction.py
python checks/verify_hub_family.py

The matching search additionally uses NumPy and SciPy. The construction stress check is separate from the theorem’s proof. The product-cover dynamic program processes each interval position exactly once and stores the least cardinality for each capped valuation state; its optima are therefore exact, without floating-point optimization.

The all-scale extension and conditional transfer were independently rederived by a separate reasoning pass, including the distinction between the target product and incidental prime factors of selected positions. This is a mathematical audit, not a Lean proof or human peer review.

What remains unresolved.

The missing step is a proof of (H2)(H_2), a sufficiently uniform weakened version implying a 2+o(1)2+o(1) fractional coefficient, or a different direct covering argument. Even proving (H2)(H_2) and applying the rounding above would leave the stronger exact 2n2n bound to be settled. The computations, special classes, and counterexamples to auxiliary rules in this note do not fill those gaps.
